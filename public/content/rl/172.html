🧩 Analogy: turning down the volume vs muting

If one person in a meeting gets too loud, you can turn their volume down, or mute them. PPO’s clip mutes a token whose ratio moved too far: its gradient becomes zero. CISPO turns the volume down: the token still contributes, with a capped weight.

2. The objective (MiniMax-M1, 2025)

\[\mathcal J_{\text{CISPO}}=\mathbb E\Big[\frac{1}{\sum_i|o_i|}\sum_i\sum_t\operatorname{sg}\big(\hat r_{i,t}\big)\,\hat A_{i,t}\,\log\pi_\theta(o_{i,t}|\cdot)\Big],\qquad\hat r_{i,t}=\operatorname{clip}\big(r_{i,t},1-\epsilon^{\text{IS}}_{\text{low}},1+\epsilon^{\text{IS}}_{\text{high}}\big).\] Group-relative advantages, token-level normalization, no KL term. The paper sets no effective lower bound (\(\epsilon^{\text{IS}}_{\text{low}}\) large) and tunes only the upper one.

Why: in MiniMax’s runs, rare reflective tokens such as “However,” “Wait,” “Recheck” and “Aha” act as forks in the reasoning. They start with low probability, so after one update their ratio is high, and PPO/GRPO clipping cut them out of every later off-policy update. CISPO keeps their gradient, with a bounded weight, which the paper credits for more stable entropy and faster training. They found DAPO’s clip-higher less effective in their setup of 16 off-policy updates per batch.

🔍 Deeper: bias for variance

Without clipping, \(\operatorname{sg}(r)\,A\nabla\log\pi=A\,\nabla r\) (since \(r\nabla\log\pi=\nabla r\)), which is the ordinary importance-weighted policy gradient. Clipping the weight caps the variance from large ratios, at the cost of a small bias. PPO instead drops those terms entirely.

🔑 Key ideas

  • PPO zeroes the gradient of over-moved tokens, while CISPO keeps it with a capped, detached weight.

  • This protects rare high-value tokens across many off-policy updates.

🚶 Walkthrough: the “Wait” token

Ratio \(1.5\), \(A=+1\). PPO with \(\epsilon=0.2\): \(\min(1.5,1.2)=1.2\) (constant), so the gradient is 0. CISPO with \(\epsilon_{\text{high}}=0.28\): weight \(\min(1.5,1.28)=1.28\) (detached), so the gradient with respect to \(\log\pi\) is \(-1.28\) in the loss, and the token keeps being reinforced.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting .detach() (the weight’s own gradient changes the estimator).

  • Clipping \(\rho A\) instead of \(\rho\).

  • No cap at all: the variance explodes on stale batches.

Solutions

import torch


def ppo_token_loss(logp_new, logp_old, adv, eps=0.2):
    ratio = (logp_new - logp_old).exp()
    return -torch.min(ratio * adv, ratio.clamp(1 - eps, 1 + eps) * adv).mean()


def cispo_loss(logp_new, logp_old, adv, mask, eps_high=0.28):
    ratio = (logp_new - logp_old).exp()
    weight = ratio.clamp(max=1 + eps_high).detach()       # clip the IS weight, then stop gradient
    return -(weight * adv * logp_new * mask).sum() / mask.sum()


# A rare reflective token ("Wait") whose probability rose 50% after the first update. A > 0.
logp_old = torch.tensor([-4.0])
logp_new = (logp_old + torch.log(torch.tensor(1.5))).requires_grad_()
adv, mask = torch.tensor([1.0]), torch.tensor([1.0])

ppo_token_loss(logp_new, logp_old, adv).backward()
print(f"PPO/GRPO grad on 'Wait': {logp_new.grad.item():.3f}")
logp_new.grad = None
cispo_loss(logp_new, logp_old, adv, mask).backward()
print(f"CISPO    grad on 'Wait': {logp_new.grad.item():.3f}")

Output:

PPO/GRPO grad on 'Wait': 0.000
CISPO    grad on 'Wait': -1.280

Check answers

  1. Ratios are relative: a small absolute increase on a tiny probability is a big ratio.

  2. Unbiasedness: the capped weight undercounts large-ratio tokens (in exchange for lower variance).

  3. Each extra update moves ratios further from 1, so more tokens cross the clip and get muted under PPO.

Retrieval answers: (1) \(A>0\) with \(\rho>1+\epsilon\), and \(A<0\) with \(\rho<1-\epsilon\). (2) It turns the factor into a constant: no gradient flows through it.

Takeaway: clipping the weight instead of the update keeps rare, important tokens learning while still bounding variance.
