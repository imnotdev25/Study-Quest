🧩 Analogy: hiking downhill in thick fog

In fog, you can see the slope right at your feet. That slope is real, but it only tells you about the next few metres. Take one giant leap in that direction and you might go off a cliff. A trust region says: “trust the local slope only within a small radius, take a step, then look again.”

The local model says “bigger step = better forever.” The truth peaks and falls. Steps are trustworthy only near the current \(\theta\) (green).

2. Why a correct gradient can still cause damage

A policy gradient gives a locally useful direction. It does not say how far to move.

Imagine adjusting a recipe after tasting one spoonful. The feedback may correctly say “add salt,” but pouring in the entire container is still disastrous.

Using data collected by an old policy \(\pi_{\text{old}}\), the surrogate objective is:

\[L(\theta) = \mathbb E_t \left[ \rho_t(\theta)\hat A_t \right]\]

where:

\[\rho_t(\theta) = \frac{ \pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t) }{ \pi_{\text{old}}(A_t\mid S_t) }\]

This objective is reliable only near the policy that generated the data. After a large update:

  • stored advantages may no longer describe the new policy well;

  • importance ratios can become extreme;

  • useful behaviours can disappear abruptly;

  • the same rollout batch becomes increasingly off-policy.

A trust region limits the update:

\[\max_\theta \quad \mathbb E_t[\rho_t(\theta)\hat A_t]\]

subject to:

\[\mathbb E_t \left[ D_{\mathrm{KL}} \left( \pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot\mid S_t) \;\|\; \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid S_t) \right) \right] \leq \delta\]

The KL divergence measures how much the complete action distribution changed:

\[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q) = \sum_a p(a)\log\frac{p(a)}{q(a)}\]

Important limits:

  • KL is asymmetric: \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)\neq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)\).

  • Small average KL does not guarantee every state changed only slightly.

  • A trust region controls update size; it does not guarantee good rewards, advantages or data.

TRPO approximately solves this constrained problem using curvature information and a line search. Today’s toy uses an ordinary gradient plus backtracking to isolate the trust-region idea.

LLM connection

A language model can forget formatting, reasoning or safety behaviours after an overly large reward-driven update. Monitoring KL from the rollout policy is therefore important.

This “old-policy KL” controls optimization stability. It is related to, but not identical to, RLHF’s KL penalty against a fixed SFT reference model.

🔍 Deeper: TRPO and the natural gradient

TRPO solves \[\max_\theta\ \mathbb E_{s,a\sim\pi_{\text{old}}}\Big[\tfrac{\pi_\theta(a|s)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(a|s)}\hat A(s,a)\Big] \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbb E_s\big[\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\text{old}}\|\pi_\theta)\big]\le\delta .\] Second-order view: near \(\theta_{\text{old}}\), \(\mathrm{KL}\approx\tfrac12\Delta^\top F\Delta\), where \(F\) is the Fisher information matrix. Maximizing \(g^\top\Delta\) under that quadratic constraint (with a Lagrangian) gives \[\Delta^*=\sqrt{\frac{2\delta}{g^\top F^{-1}g}}\;F^{-1}g .\] This is the natural gradient: it measures step size in “how much the policy’s behaviour changes,” not in raw parameter distance. A backtracking line search then shrinks the step until KL and improvement both check out (your exercise).

🔑 Key ideas

  • A gradient gives a direction. A trust region decides how far to believe it.

  • Measure steps in KL (behaviour), not in parameter units.

🚶 Walkthrough: backtracking

KL limit \(\delta=0.01\). The full natural step gives KL \(0.04\). KL grows roughly with the square of step size, so halving gives about \(0.01\), which is accepted. If the surrogate had dropped at the half step, halve again (KL \(\approx0.0025\)) and recheck.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring KL in the wrong direction or on a different batch.

  • Accepting a step that satisfies KL but makes the surrogate worse.

  • Recomputing advantages between line-search tries.

⚙️ Algorithm: Trust-region step with backtracking line search
Input: current \(\theta_{\text{old}}\), proposed step \(\Delta\) (e.g. natural gradient), KL limit \(\delta\), shrink factor \(c\in(0,1)\), max tries \(J\)
for \(j=0,1,\dots,J\) do
\(\theta\gets\theta_{\text{old}}+c^j\Delta\)
if \(\overline{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}\|\pi_\theta)\le\delta\) and surrogate \(L(\theta)>L(\theta_{\text{old}})\) then
return \(\theta\) ▷ accept the largest safe step
end
end
return \(\theta_{\text{old}}\) ▷ no safe improving step found

3. PyTorch exercise — backtrack until KL is safe

Solutions

Importance ratios and surrogate:

def surrogate(logits):
    policy = Categorical(logits=logits)

    ratios = (
        policy.log_prob(actions)
        - old_log_probs
    ).exp()

    return (
        ratios * advantages
    ).mean()

Gradient direction:

objective = surrogate(new_logits)
loss = -objective

loss.backward()

ascent_direction = -new_logits.grad.detach()

Aggressive KL:

aggressive_kl = kl_divergence(
    old_policy,
    aggressive_policy,
)

Candidate measurements:

candidate_kl = kl_divergence(
    old_policy,
    candidate_policy,
)

candidate_objective = surrogate(
    candidate_logits
).detach()

Backtracking:

step_size = step_size * 0.5

The accepted policy should increase the probability of action 2, but only modestly because the KL budget is small.

The initial surrogate is approximately zero:

\[\frac1B\sum_t\hat A_t\approx0\]

A valid accepted candidate should have:

candidate KL <= 0.01
candidate surrogate >= initial surrogate

The aggressive candidate will generally have a much larger KL and be rejected. Exact probabilities depend on sampled rewards, but the fixed seed makes the run reproducible.

This toy performs line search along an ordinary gradient. TRPO instead scales directions using policy curvature because an equal Euclidean change in parameters need not produce an equal change in action probabilities.

Check answers

  1. The stored actions and advantages describe \(\pi_{\text{old}}\); after a large change, they poorly represent the new policy’s trajectories.

  2. No. It only says the policy stayed nearby.

  3. KL checks update size, while the surrogate checks whether the sampled local objective improved.

  4. It limits how much extreme probability ratios can influence a policy update, avoiding TRPO’s more expensive constrained optimization.

Retrieval answers

  1. \(\rho_t=\exp(\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)-\log\pi_{\text{old}}(A_t\mid S_t))\).

  2. A small number of heavily weighted samples dominate the estimate.

Takeaway: the gradient suggests a direction; a trust region decides how far that suggestion remains believable. This is the bridge from importance ratios to PPO.
