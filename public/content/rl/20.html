🧩 Analogy: don’t eat the same meal every day

Even if pizza is your favourite, eating only pizza means you’ll never notice if your tastes change or a better option opens nearby. Investors call it diversification. Entropy regularization pays the agent a small bonus for keeping options open, so it doesn’t commit to one action too early.

2. Reward alone can collapse a policy

Suppose several routes reach the gym, but one currently looks slightly faster. A reward-only policy may commit to it immediately and stop learning about the alternatives.

Entropy measures how spread out a categorical policy is:

\[\mathcal H(\pi(\cdot\mid s)) = -\sum_a \pi(a\mid s)\log\pi(a\mid s)\]

  • High entropy: probabilities are spread across actions.

  • Low entropy: one action dominates.

  • Deterministic policy: entropy approaches zero.

Entropy-regularized RL maximizes:

\[J(\pi) = \mathbb E_\pi[R] + \alpha\mathcal H(\pi)\]

where \(\alpha\geq0\) controls the reward–exploration trade-off.

Because:

\[\mathcal H(\pi) = \mathbb E_{a\sim\pi} [-\log\pi(a\mid s)]\]

we can interpret the objective as maximizing a modified reward:

\[R_{\text{soft}} = R-\alpha\log\pi(a\mid s)\]

A surprising action has low probability and therefore large \(-\log\pi\), giving it an exploration bonus.

The soft optimal policy

For known action values \(Q(s,a)\), the entropy-regularized optimum is:

\[\pi^*(a\mid s) = \frac{\exp(Q(s,a)/\alpha)} {\sum_j\exp(Q(s,j)/\alpha)}\]

This is a Boltzmann or softmax policy.

  • Small \(\alpha\): sharply prefers the best action.

  • Large \(\alpha\): more uniform.

  • As \(\alpha\to0\): approaches greedy selection.

The corresponding soft value is:

\[V_{\text{soft}}(s) = \alpha\log \sum_a\exp\left(\frac{Q(s,a)}{\alpha}\right)\]

Use softmax and logsumexp rather than manually exponentiating large logits.

For a batch:

  • logits and action probabilities: [B, actions]

  • selected log-probabilities: [B]

  • entropy per state: [B]

  • objective: scalar

What entropy does not guarantee

Entropy creates diverse actions, but not necessarily useful exploration. A policy can repeatedly sample irrelevant or unsafe actions while remaining highly entropic.

A large \(\alpha\) can also prevent convergence to a task’s best deterministic behaviour.

LLM connection

A token-level entropy bonus discourages an LLM from becoming prematurely deterministic. But excessive entropy can increase incoherent or low-quality tokens. Prompt and padding tokens must be excluded when averaging token entropy.

SAC, coming next, builds entropy directly into its actor and critic targets.

🔍 Deeper: the soft-optimal policy is a softmax

At one state, maximize \(\sum_a\pi(a)Q(a)+\alpha\mathcal H(\pi)\) subject to \(\sum_a\pi(a)=1\). Setting the derivative of the Lagrangian to zero: \[Q(a)-\alpha(\log\pi(a)+1)-\lambda=0\ \Rightarrow\ \pi^*(a)=\frac{\exp(Q(a)/\alpha)}{\sum_b\exp(Q(b)/\alpha)} .\] The optimal value is the soft max: \(V=\alpha\log\sum_a e^{Q(a)/\alpha}\). As \(\alpha\to0\) this becomes \(\max_aQ(a)\) (greedy), and as \(\alpha\to\infty\) the policy becomes uniform.

Low temperature: almost greedy. High temperature: heading toward uniform (1/3 each).

🔑 Key ideas

  • Objective \(=\) reward \(+\ \alpha\cdot\)entropy, and its optimum is a Boltzmann (softmax) policy.

  • \(\alpha\) is the exchange rate between reward and curiosity.

🚶 Walkthrough: temperature in numbers

\(Q=(2,1,0)\).

  • \(\alpha=1\): \(\pi=(0.665,0.245,0.090)\), soft value \(V=\log(e^2+e+1)=2.408\).

  • \(\alpha=0.5\): \(\pi=(0.867,0.117,0.016)\). Closer to greedy.

\(V\) exceeds \(\max Q=2\): the soft max adds a bonus for having options.

Common mistakes

  • Adding \(-\alpha\mathcal H\) instead of \(+\alpha\mathcal H\) to the objective.

  • Forgetting \(\alpha\) inside the exponent.

  • Computing \(\log\sum e^{x}\) naively (overflow), instead of with logsumexp.

⚙️ Algorithm: Soft value iteration (entropy-regularized)
Input: transitions \(P\), rewards \(r\), discount \(\gamma\), temperature \(\alpha\)
\(V(s)\gets0\) for every state
repeat
\(Q(s,a)\gets r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'|s,a)V(s')\) for all \(s,a\)
\(V(s)\gets\alpha\log\sum_a\exp\big(Q(s,a)/\alpha\big)\) ▷ soft max replaces max
until \(V\) stops changing
\(\pi(a|s)\gets\exp\big((Q(s,a)-V(s))/\alpha\big)\) ▷ a softmax policy

Debugging experiment

Use the wrong entropy sign:

objective = expected_reward - alpha * entropy

This penalizes uncertainty. The policy collapses toward the best action faster instead of retaining controlled exploration.

Then try:

alpha = 5.0

The policy becomes much closer to uniform. It explores more, but sacrifices expected environmental reward.

4. Checks for understanding

  1. Why is \(-\log\pi(a\mid s)\) an exploration bonus?

  2. What happens as \(\alpha\rightarrow0\)?

  3. Why might a high-entropy policy still explore poorly?

  4. Why should entropy be tracked separately from task reward?

Solutions

Probabilities and expected reward:

probabilities = policy.probs

expected_reward = (
    probabilities * rewards
).sum()

Entropy:

entropy = policy.entropy()

Objective and loss:

objective = (
    expected_reward
    + alpha * entropy
)

loss = -objective

Closed-form solution:

theoretical_probabilities = torch.softmax(
    rewards / alpha,
    dim=0,
)

Soft value:

soft_value = alpha * torch.logsumexp(
    rewards / alpha,
    dim=0,
)

With \(\alpha=0.5\):

\[\pi^* = \operatorname{softmax}([0,2,4])\]

Approximately:

action 0: 0.016
action 1: 0.117
action 2: 0.867

The best action dominates, but its probability remains below one because entropy is part of the objective.

Initially, the policy is uniform, so:

\[\mathcal H(\pi) = -\sum_{a=1}^{3}\frac13\log\frac13 = \log3 \approx1.099\]

The soft value is:

\[V_{\text{soft}} = 0.5\log \left( e^0+e^2+e^4 \right) \approx2.071\]

It exceeds the maximum environmental reward because it includes the entropy bonus. This does not mean the agent receives more than reward 2 from the environment.

The learned probabilities should closely match the theoretical probabilities. Small differences can remain due to finite optimization steps.

Check answers

  1. Rare actions have more negative log-probabilities, so \(-\log\pi\) is larger and rewards sampling them.

  2. The entropy term disappears and the soft policy approaches a greedy policy.

  3. Entropy rewards diversity, not relevance, safety or information gain.

  4. The combined objective can improve because entropy rises even while actual task performance falls.

Retrieval answers

  1. PPO stops rewarding further probability increases through the clipped surrogate term.

  2. Clipping limits individual objective incentives but does not impose a hard constraint on the complete policy distribution.

Takeaway: ordinary RL asks for high reward. Entropy-regularized RL asks for high reward while retaining useful uncertainty. The temperature \(\alpha\) determines how much reward the policy will trade for continued exploration.
