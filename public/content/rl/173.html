🧩 Analogy: a restaurant regular who stops trying new dishes

After a few great meals you order the same dish every time. That’s efficient, until the menu changes or a better dish exists that you’ll never discover. A reasoning model under RL does the same: it quickly concentrates probability on the patterns that paid off early, entropy collapses, and accuracy plateaus because nothing new gets tried.

Stylized \(R=-a\,e^{H}+b\) (Cui et al., 2025). Performance is bought with entropy, so once entropy is gone, so is further progress.

2. The mechanism (Cui et al., 2025, “The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning LMs”)

Empirical law: without intervention, entropy drops sharply early and performance follows \(R\approx-a\,e^{H}+b\), so \(H\to0\) implies a predictable ceiling \(b-a\).

What drives it: for a softmax policy, the change in entropy after one policy-gradient step is approximately \[H_{k+1}-H_k\approx-\eta\,\operatorname{Cov}_{a\sim\pi}\big(\log\pi(a|s),\ A(s,a)\big)\] (the natural-gradient form; vanilla PG has a similar covariance with \(\pi(a)A\)). If the actions the policy already likes also get positive advantages, which is the usual case, the covariance is positive and entropy falls.

Fixes from the paper: find the tokens with the highest covariance \((\log\pi-\overline{\log\pi})(A-\bar A)\), which are a small fraction, and either

  • Clip-Cov: detach them from the gradient, or

  • KL-Cov: add a KL penalty only on them.

🔑 Key ideas

  • Entropy falls because confident tokens keep getting positive advantages.

  • Restrict updates on the highest-covariance tokens to keep exploring.

🚶 Walkthrough: which tokens are high-covariance?

Four tokens with \((\log\pi,A)\): \((-0.1,+1)\), \((-3.0,+1)\), \((-0.2,-1)\), \((-2.5,-1)\). Means: \(\overline{\log\pi}=-1.45\), \(\bar A=0\). Covariance terms: \((1.35)(1)=1.35\), \((-1.55)(1)=-1.55\), \((1.25)(-1)=-1.25\), \((-1.05)(-1)=1.05\). The top term is the confident token being rewarded, exactly the update that sharpens the policy. The rare token being rewarded has negative covariance: it increases entropy, so keep it.

Common mistakes

  • A global entropy bonus large enough to make outputs random.

  • Detaching a large fraction (you lose most of the signal).

  • Reading low entropy as “converged” when accuracy has plateaued.

4. Checks for understanding

  1. Why does a positive covariance between \(\log\pi\) and \(A\) lower entropy?

  2. In the bandit, Clip-Cov gets lower reward. Why is that expected here, and why can it still help on reasoning tasks?

Solutions

import torch


def run(clip_cov, steps=150, seed=0):
    torch.manual_seed(seed)
    k = 10
    true_reward = torch.linspace(0.0, 1.0, k)          # arm 9 is best
    logits = torch.zeros(k, requires_grad=True)
    opt = torch.optim.SGD([logits], lr=0.5)
    for _ in range(steps):
        probs = logits.softmax(-1)
        a = torch.multinomial(probs.detach(), 64, replacement=True)
        r = (torch.rand(64) < true_reward[a]).float()   # Bernoulli reward
        adv = (r - r.mean()) / (r.std() + 1e-6)
        logp = logits.log_softmax(-1)[a]
        keep = torch.ones(64)
        if clip_cov:
            cov = (logp.detach() - logp.detach().mean()) * (adv - adv.mean())
            top = cov.topk(int(0.2 * 64)).indices       # highest-covariance 20%
            keep[top] = 0.0                             # detach them from the update
        loss = -(keep * adv * logp).sum() / keep.sum()
        opt.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        opt.step()
    p = logits.softmax(-1).detach()
    entropy = -(p * p.log()).sum().item()
    return entropy, (p * true_reward).sum().item()


for clip_cov in (False, True):
    ent, rew = zip(*(run(clip_cov, seed=s) for s in range(5)))
    print(f"clip_cov={clip_cov!s:5}: entropy {sum(ent)/5:.3f}  expected reward {sum(rew)/5:.3f}")

Output:

clip_cov=False: entropy 0.053  expected reward 0.998
clip_cov=True : entropy 0.792  expected reward 0.935

Check answers

  1. Probability flows toward actions that are already likely, which sharpens the distribution.

  2. A bandit has nothing left to discover once the best arm is found, so exploration is pure cost. In reasoning, a collapsed policy stops finding new solution paths, and keeping entropy is what lets accuracy keep rising.

Retrieval answers: (1) Rewards spread-out policies, slowing collapse. (2) Rare tokens hit a symmetric clip almost immediately.

Takeaway: track entropy alongside reward. When it collapses, progress stalls, and the culprits are a small set of confident, rewarded tokens.
