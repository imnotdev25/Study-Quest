2. Optimizing a proxy is not the same as optimizing the goal

Suppose the real objective is human utility \(U(x,y)\), but training can only observe a proxy reward \(R(x,y)\):

\[\underbrace{ \max_\theta E_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[R(x,y)] }_{\text{what training optimizes}} \qquad\neq\qquad \underbrace{ \max_\theta E_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[U(x,y)] }_{\text{what we actually want}}\]

Reward hacking occurs when the policy finds outputs with high proxy reward but poor real utility.

Examples:

  • code passes incomplete tests without solving the task;

  • a judge rewards verbosity, citations or confident tone;

  • a safety model learns a refusal template rather than safe reasoning;

  • a financial assistant exploits evaluator keywords while giving bad advice;

  • a tool agent reports success without verifying the tool result.

Reference-KL regularization can slow exploitation:

\[J(\pi) = \underbrace{E_\pi[R(a)]}_{\text{proxy reward}} - \underbrace{ \beta D_{\mathrm{KL}} (\pi\Vert\pi_{\text{ref}}) }_{\text{drift cost}}\]

For an unconstrained categorical policy, the optimum has the form:

\[\pi^*(a) = \underbrace{ \frac{ \pi_{\text{ref}}(a) \exp(R(a)/\beta) }{ \sum_{a'} \pi_{\text{ref}}(a') \exp(R(a')/\beta) } }_{\text{reference probability tilted exponentially by proxy reward}}\]

Even an initially rare exploit can dominate when its proxy reward is sufficiently larger. KL does not determine whether the reward is correct.

Training therefore needs independent diagnostics:

  • training reward;

  • held-out verifier or human audit score;

  • policy entropy and KL;

  • reward components separately;

  • output lengths and strategy frequencies;

  • adversarial tests for evaluator shortcuts.

DPO is not immune. If preference labels reward sycophancy, verbosity or evaluator-specific artifacts, DPO learns those artifacts offline. It lacks online exploration, but it still optimizes a proxy dataset.

Shapes for today’s toy model:

  • strategy rewards and policy logits: [A]

  • recorded metrics: [checkpoints, 5 + A]

  • losses, expected rewards, KL and entropy: scalar []

Initially, the reference policy’s audit utility is:

\[\begin{aligned} E_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}[U] &= \underbrace{ 0.60(1.0) +0.15(0.4) +0.05(-0.5) +0.20(0.2) }_{\text{reference-weighted true utility}}\\ &=0.675 \end{aligned}\]

Under the flawed verifier, the exploit receives the largest reward. Training therefore raises its probability, increasing the reported training reward while sharply reducing audit utility.

The hardened verifier assigns the exploit negative reward. Its policy should instead concentrate heavily on the honest solution.

Exact final values depend slightly on optimizer convergence, but the expected pattern is:

  • flawed training reward: rises;

  • flawed audit utility: falls;

  • flawed exploit probability: becomes large;

  • hardened exploit probability: approaches zero;

  • hardened audit utility: rises.

Check answers

  1. KL charges for moving away from the reference, but proxy reward enters exponentially. A sufficiently large proxy advantage can outweigh a low reference probability.

  2. Optimized reward rises while an independent human, verifier or deployment metric stagnates or declines—often alongside growing disagreement and strategy concentration.

  3. No. Unit tests are still a specification. Missing cases, parser behaviour, timeouts and numerical tolerances can all become exploits.

  4. If training repeatedly adapts to the audit, it stops measuring generalization and becomes another component of the optimized proxy.

Retrieval answers

  1. GRPO replaces the critic with the sampled reward mean and variance of completions answering the same prompt.

  2. \(\pi_{\text{old}}\) defines the importance ratio; \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) limits accumulated drift from the reference/SFT policy.

Takeaway: a rising reward proves only that the policy learned the scoring rule. Trust improvement only when independent audits, held-out verifiers and real task metrics rise with it.
