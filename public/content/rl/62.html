🧩 Analogy: a phone plan with adaptive overage pricing

Imagine a data plan where, if you go over your limit this month, next month’s per-GB price rises, and if you stay well under, it falls. You’d naturally settle near your limit. A Lagrange multiplier is that adaptive price for tool calls: instead of guessing a fixed penalty, let measured violations set it.

Stylized: calls overshoot the budget (2), \(\lambda\) rises, and calls settle at the budget.

2. Success and resource limits are different objectives

A tool agent may need to maximize task reward while respecting a limit:

\[\max_\pi \mathbb E_\pi[R]\]

subject to:

\[\mathbb E_\pi[C]\le B\]

Here, \(C\) might represent tool calls, latency, money or risk, and \(B\) is the allowed budget.

A manually chosen objective such as:

\[R-\beta C\]

requires guessing \(\beta\). A Lagrangian approach adapts the penalty:

\[\mathcal L(\pi,\lambda) = \mathbb E_\pi[R] - \lambda \left( \mathbb E_\pi[C]-B \right)\]

with:

\[\lambda\ge0\]

The policy maximizes \(\mathcal L\). The multiplier follows dual ascent:

\[\lambda \leftarrow \max\left( 0,\; \lambda+\eta_\lambda (\mathbb E[C]-B) \right)\]

Therefore:

  • cost above budget increases \(\lambda\);

  • cost below budget decreases \(\lambda\);

  • \(\lambda=0\) when the constraint is comfortably satisfied.

This separates the real reward from operational constraints. It also makes the trade-off observable rather than hiding it inside one shaped score.

Practical cautions:

  • measure cost from the environment, not model claims;

  • use comparable units and stable averaging windows;

  • dual updates that are too fast can oscillate;

  • per-batch constraints can be noisy;

  • evaluate hard safety constraints outside this expected-cost formulation.

Tensor shapes:

  • strategy logits, probabilities, rewards and costs: [A]

  • expected reward, cost and entropy: scalar

  • multiplier \(\lambda\): nonnegative scalar

DPO preferences may indirectly encode a preference for cheaper traces, but ordinary DPO cannot adapt a multiplier from live deployment costs.

🔍 Deeper: dual ascent

Goal: \(\max_\pi\mathbb E[R]\) subject to \(\mathbb E[C]\le d\). Lagrangian: \(\mathcal L(\pi,\lambda)=\mathbb E[R]-\lambda(\mathbb E[C]-d)\), with \(\lambda\ge0\). Alternate: (1) improve \(\pi\) on \(R-\lambda C\); (2) \(\lambda\gets\max(0,\lambda+\eta_\lambda(\hat C-d))\). At a solution, \(\lambda\) is the shadow price: how much reward one extra unit of budget would buy. The full algorithm is in Topic 151.

🔑 Key ideas

  • Treat success and cost as separate signals.

  • Let violations tune the price, and monitor reward, cost and \(\lambda\) separately.

🚶 Walkthrough: three \(\lambda\) updates

Budget \(d=2\), \(\eta_\lambda=0.1\), \(\lambda_0=0\). Measured calls \(3.5,2.4,1.8\): \(\lambda=0+0.1(1.5)=0.15\), then \(0.15+0.04=0.19\), then \(0.19-0.02=0.17\).

Common mistakes

  • Backpropagating into \(\lambda\) through the policy loss.

  • Letting \(\lambda\) go negative (that rewards spending).

  • Estimating cost from a handful of rollouts (noisy \(\lambda\)).

3. PyTorch exercise — meet a tool-call budget

  • direct answer: moderate success, no tool cost;

  • one tool: high success, cost 1;

Hints

  • Use logits.softmax(dim=-1).

  • Expectations are probability-weighted sums.

  • Categorical(probs=probabilities).entropy() is scalar.

  • Maximize reward minus lambda * violation plus entropy.

  • PyTorch optimizers minimize, so negate the objective.

  • Project the multiplier using .clamp_min(0.0).

Debugging experiments

Use gradient descent for \(\lambda\). When cost exceeds budget, the penalty decreases—the opposite of the desired controller.

Remove .detach() from the dual measurement while implementing \(\lambda\) as a differentiable parameter. Actor and dual gradients can become accidentally coupled.

Increase dual_lr to 1.0. The multiplier may overshoot, causing the policy to alternate between expensive and overly conservative behavior.

Finally, evaluate only the combined Lagrangian. A stable combined number can hide declining reward, rising cost or cancellation between them.

Solutions

Strategy probabilities:

probabilities = logits.softmax(
    dim=-1
)

Expected quantities:

expected_reward = (
    probabilities * rewards
).sum()

expected_cost = (
    probabilities * costs
).sum()

entropy = Categorical(
    probs=probabilities
).entropy()

Policy objective:

policy_objective = (
    expected_reward
    - lambda_value.detach()
    * constraint_violation
    + entropy_coefficient
    * entropy
)

policy_loss = -policy_objective

Policy update:

optimizer.zero_grad()
policy_loss.backward()
optimizer.step()

Dual update:

with torch.no_grad():
    lambda_value = (
        lambda_value
        + dual_lr
        * constraint_violation.detach()
    ).clamp_min(0.0)

Unconstrained optimization selects action 2:

\[R_2=1.0,\qquad C_2=3\]

which violates:

\[B=1.2\]

The constrained policy can mix strategies. It may retain some probability on action 2, provided cheaper choices keep average cost near the budget:

\[\sum_a\pi(a)C(a)\lesssim1.2\]

Entropy regularization and finite optimization mean the final cost may not equal 1.2 exactly.

Check answers

  1. The multiplier solves a maximization problem: increase the penalty when the constraint is violated. The actor separately maximizes its penalized objective.

  2. The policy continues violating the constraint, the dual learning rate is too slow, or the constraint may be infeasible.

  3. A low average can hide rare catastrophic calls. Hard permissions, per-episode limits and tail-risk constraints may also be required.

  4. Log raw task reward, each cost component, constraint violation, multiplier, policy entropy, tool frequencies and cost percentiles—not only their weighted sum.

Retrieval answers

  1. It changes the optimized return, so extra calls can become profitable even when they do not improve task success.

  2. Intermediate potential terms cancel when shaping and return calculations use matching discount factors and consistent endpoint potentials.

Takeaway: treat success and tool cost as separate signals. Let the multiplier respond to measured constraint violations, and monitor reward, cost and \(\lambda\) independently.
