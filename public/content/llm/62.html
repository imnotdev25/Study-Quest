🚀 Starting point

KL divergence \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)=\sum_ap(a)\log\frac{p(a)}{q(a)}\) measures how much \(p\) differs from \(q\). It’s \(\ge0\) and equals 0 only if \(p=q\). In LLM training it’s computed per token between the policy’s next-token distribution and the reference model’s, then averaged over valid tokens.

Total: 20 minutes

1. Retrieval + concept refresh — 5 minutes

Quick retrieval:

  1. In DPO, why is the reference model frozen?

  2. Why can an individual sampled log-ratio be negative even if KL divergence is nonnegative?

For policy and reference distributions over vocabulary tokens:

\[\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s),\quad \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid s) \in\mathbb{R}^{V}\]

the forward KL divergence is:

\[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\text{ref}}) = \sum_{a=1}^{V} \pi_\theta(a\mid s) \left[ \log\pi_\theta(a\mid s) - \log\pi_{\text{ref}}(a\mid s) \right]\]

For LM logits:

\[Z_\theta,Z_{\text{ref}} \in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T\times V}\]

the exact token-level KL has shape:

\[K\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T}\]

A masked mean is:

\[L_{\mathrm{KL}} = \frac{\sum_{b,t}M_{bt}K_{bt}} {\sum_{b,t}M_{bt}}\]

If actions are sampled from the policy:

\[a\sim\pi_\theta\]

then:

\[\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_\theta} \left[ \log\pi_\theta(a)-\log\pi_{\text{ref}}(a) \right] = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\text{ref}})\]

One sampled log-ratio may be negative. Only its expectation is guaranteed to equal the nonnegative KL.

In RL-style post-training, a simplified objective is:

\[L=-\mathbb E[r]+\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\]

The KL term controls policy drift. Too little regularization can destabilize behavior; too much can prevent useful learning.

✏️ Derivation: KL is nonnegative, and its gradient with respect to the policy’s logits

Step 1. Nonnegativity: \(\log\) is concave, so by Jensen \(-D_{\mathrm{KL}}=\sum_pp\log\frac qp\le\log\sum_pp\frac qp=\log\sum q=0\).

Step 2. Let \(p=\operatorname{softmax}(z)\) be the policy, \(q\) the fixed reference, and \(\ell_a=\log p_a-\log q_a\). Then \(D=\sum_ap_a\ell_a\).

Step 3. Pieces: \(\frac{\partial p_a}{\partial z_c}=p_a(\delta_{ac}-p_c)\) and \(\frac{\partial\log p_a}{\partial z_c}=\delta_{ac}-p_c\).

Step 4. Product rule: \(\frac{\partial D}{\partial z_c}=\sum_ap_a(\delta_{ac}-p_c)\ell_a+\sum_ap_a(\delta_{ac}-p_c)=p_c\ell_c-p_cD+(p_c-p_c)\).

Step 5. So \(\boxed{\frac{\partial D}{\partial z_c}=p_c\big(\ell_c-D\big)}\): tokens where the policy is more confident than the reference (relative to the average log-ratio) are pushed down.

Step 6. Masked mean over tokens: \(L_{\mathrm{KL}}=\frac{\sum_{b,t}M_{bt}D_{bt}}{\sum M}\). Padding positions must not enter the numerator or the denominator.

⚙️ Algorithm: exact token-level KL from logits
Input: policy logits \(Z_\theta\), reference logits \(Z_{\text{ref}}\) (no gradient), mask \(M\)
\(\log p\gets\operatorname{log\_softmax}(Z_\theta)\); \(\log q\gets\operatorname{log\_softmax}(Z_{\text{ref}})\)
\(D_{bt}\gets\sum_ae^{\log p_{bta}}(\log p_{bta}-\log q_{bta})\)
\(L_{\mathrm{KL}}\gets\sum M\,D/\sum M\)

Monte Carlo estimate:

with torch.no_grad():
    distribution = torch.distributions.Categorical(
        logits=policy_logits
    )

    actions = distribution.sample((num_samples,))

    expanded_policy_logp = policy_logp.unsqueeze(0).expand(
        num_samples, -1, -1, -1
    )

    expanded_reference_logp = reference_logp.unsqueeze(0).expand(
        num_samples, -1, -1, -1
    )

    sampled_policy_logp = expanded_policy_logp.gather(
        dim=-1,
        index=actions.unsqueeze(-1),
    ).squeeze(-1)

    sampled_reference_logp = expanded_reference_logp.gather(
        dim=-1,
        index=actions.unsqueeze(-1),
    ).squeeze(-1)

    sampled_log_ratio = (
        sampled_policy_logp - sampled_reference_logp
    )

    monte_carlo_kl = sampled_log_ratio[
        :, valid_mask
    ].mean()

Individual log-ratios need not be positive. If the reference assigns more probability than the policy to a sampled action:

\[\log\pi_\theta(a)-\log\pi_{\text{ref}}(a)<0\]

The expectation under policy samples is nevertheless the nonnegative forward KL.

Masked positions do not contribute to exact_kl, so no computation-graph path connects their logits to the scalar objective. Their gradients are zero.

Forward and reverse KL differ because KL is asymmetric:

\[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)\neq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)\]

Forward KL weights outcomes according to the policy; reverse KL weights them according to the reference. They therefore penalize distributional differences differently.

Check answers:

  1. The policy: \[\sum_a\pi_\theta(a) [\log\pi_\theta(a)-\log\pi_{\text{ref}}(a)]\]

  2. Softmax is invariant to adding the same constant to every logit. The gradient has no component in that all-ones direction.

  3. Zero. Softmax removes a uniform logit shift, so the distributions are identical.

  4. Actions may have been sampled from the reference. Then: \[\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_{\text{ref}}} [\log\pi_\theta(a)-\log\pi_{\text{ref}}(a)] = -D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{\text{ref}}\|\pi_\theta)\]

  5. A larger coefficient restricts movement away from the reference, reducing destructive policy updates but also opposing reward-driven changes.

Retrieval answers:

  • The reference is a fixed behavioral anchor; gradients should update only the policy.

  • KL is an expectation. Individual Monte Carlo terms can be negative even though their correctly sampled expectation is nonnegative.
