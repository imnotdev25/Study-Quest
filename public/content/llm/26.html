🚀 Starting point

Signal propagation: at initialization, we want the size of activations (forward) and gradients (backward) to stay roughly constant through depth. Measure size by the second moment \(\mathbb E[z^2]\). Weights are random with mean 0 and variance \(\operatorname{Var}(W)\), the one number we get to choose.

Total: 20 minutes

1. Retrieval + concept refresh — 5 minutes

Quick retrieval:

  1. If \(\Delta=\partial L/\partial Z\) and \(Z=XW+b\), what is \(\partial L/\partial W\)?

  2. Why must momentum state persist across training steps?

An \(L\)-layer MLP computes:

\[H_0=X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times d_0}\]

\[Z_\ell=H_{\ell-1}W_\ell+b_\ell, \qquad W_\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\ell-1}\times d_\ell}\]

\[H_\ell=\operatorname{ReLU}(Z_\ell) \in\mathbb{R}^{B\times d_\ell}\]

For ReLU:

\[\operatorname{ReLU}(z)=\max(0,z), \qquad \frac{\partial H_\ell}{\partial Z_\ell} =\mathbf{1}[Z_\ell>0]\]

If weights are too small, activations and gradients shrink through depth. If too large, they grow until training becomes unstable.

Assuming independent, zero-mean inputs and weights:

\[\mathbb{E}[Z_\ell^2] \approx d_{\ell-1}\operatorname{Var}(W_\ell) \mathbb{E}[H_{\ell-1}^2]\]

ReLU removes roughly half the squared signal, motivating Kaiming initialization:

\[\operatorname{Var}(W_\ell)=\frac{2}{d_{\ell-1}}, \qquad \operatorname{std}(W_\ell)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{d_{\ell-1}}}\]

The goal is not identical activations at every layer, but preventing systematic exponential collapse or explosion.

✏️ Derivation: Kaiming initialization \(\operatorname{Var}(W)=2/d_{\text{in}}\)

Step 1. One pre-activation: \(z_j=\sum_{i=1}^{d}h_iW_{ij}\), with \(W_{ij}\) independent of \(h\), mean 0.

Step 2. Variance of a sum of independent zero-mean terms: \(\operatorname{Var}(z_j)=\sum_i\mathbb E[h_i^2]\,\mathbb E[W_{ij}^2]=d\,\operatorname{Var}(W)\,\mathbb E[h^2]\).

Step 3. ReLU of a zero-mean symmetric \(z\): \(\mathbb E[\max(0,z)^2]=\frac12\mathbb E[z^2]\) (the negative half is zeroed, and the positive half carries exactly half the second moment).

Step 4. Combine for layer \(\ell\): \(\operatorname{Var}(z_\ell)=d_{\ell-1}\operatorname{Var}(W_\ell)\cdot\frac12\operatorname{Var}(z_{\ell-1})\).

Step 5. After \(L\) layers: \(\operatorname{Var}(z_L)=\Big(\frac{d\operatorname{Var}(W)}{2}\Big)^L\operatorname{Var}(z_0)\). Anything other than a factor of 1 explodes or vanishes exponentially in depth.

Step 6. Set the factor to 1: \(\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(W)=2/d_{\text{in}}}\). Example: \(d=512\) gives std \(\sqrt{2/512}=0.0625\).

Step 7. Backward has the same structure with \(d_{\text{out}}\), so “fan-out” Kaiming uses \(2/d_{\text{out}}\). Xavier (\(1/d\) or \(2/(d_{\text{in}}+d_{\text{out}})\)) is the version for tanh, which doesn’t halve the signal.

⚙️ Algorithm: Kaiming-normal initialization of a ReLU MLP
Input: layer widths \(d_0,\dots,d_L\)
for \(\ell=1,\dots,L\) do
\(W_\ell\sim\mathcal N\big(0,\ 2/d_{\ell-1}\big)\) entrywise; \(b_\ell\gets0\)
end
check: forward a random batch and log \(\operatorname{std}(Z_\ell)\) per layer; it should stay roughly flat

Solution — open after attempting

def run_mlp(gain):
    weights = []

    for _ in range(depth):
        std = gain / math.sqrt(D)
        W = (torch.randn(D, D) * std).requires_grad_()
        weights.append(W)

    X = X_base.clone().requires_grad_()
    h = X
    activation_rms = []

    for W in weights:
        z = h @ W
        h = torch.relu(z)
        activation_rms.append(
            h.square().mean().sqrt().item()
        )

    probe = torch.randn_like(h) / math.sqrt(h.numel())
    loss = (h * probe).sum()
    loss.backward()

    input_grad_rms = X.grad.square().mean().sqrt().item()
    return activation_rms, input_grad_rms

Expected pattern:

  • small: activation RMS progressively shrinks.

  • kaiming: activation scale is approximately preserved.

  • large: activation RMS progressively grows.

With relu(z - 5), most preactivations fall below the threshold. The active fraction approaches zero, and the corresponding ReLU derivatives become zero. This can eliminate the gradient flowing to earlier layers.

Check answers:

  1. Both \(Z_\ell\) and its upstream gradient have shape [B, d_out]. The weight gradient is:

    \[\frac{\partial L}{\partial W_\ell} = H_{\ell-1}^{\top} \frac{\partial L}{\partial Z_\ell} \in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{in}}\times d_{\text{out}}}\]

  2. ReLU sets roughly half its inputs to zero, so the factor of two compensates for the lost squared signal.

  3. Approximately:

    \[0.8^{10}\approx0.107\]

    Only about \(11\%\) remains.

  4. Inspect the activation mean, preactivation distribution, active-unit fraction, and per-neuron statistics. RMS alone can hide a large positive shift.

  5. Backward propagation also depends on weight transposes, activation derivatives, residual paths, loss scaling, and correlations ignored by the initialization assumptions.
