🚀 Starting point

Starting point. The expectation \(\mathbb E[g(X)]=\sum_xg(x)p(x)\) or \(\int g(x)f(x)dx\) is a probability-weighted average. The \(k\)-th moment is \(\mathbb E[X^k]\); the variance \(\operatorname{Var}X=\mathbb E[(X-\mathbb EX)^2]\) measures spread, and its square root, the standard deviation, is in the same units as \(X\).

✏️ Derivation: The rules of expectation and variance

Step 1. Linearity: \(\mathbb E[aX+bY+c]=a\mathbb EX+b\mathbb EY+c\), always, even for dependent \(X,Y\) (the integral is linear).

Step 2. Expand the square: \(\operatorname{Var}X=\mathbb E[X^2]-2(\mathbb EX)^2+(\mathbb EX)^2=\mathbb E[X^2]-(\mathbb EX)^2\).

Step 3. \(\operatorname{Var}(aX+c)=a^2\operatorname{Var}X\): shifting doesn’t change spread; scaling multiplies it by \(a^2\).

Step 4. \(\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}X+\operatorname{Var}Y+2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)\), so variances add for independent variables. Averaging \(n\) independent copies divides the variance by \(n\): the reason more samples help.

  • Jensen: for convex \(\phi\), \(\phi(\mathbb EX)\le\mathbb E\phi(X)\) (reverse for concave). \(\mathbb E\log X\le\log\mathbb EX\) is why the ELBO is a lower bound, and why \(\mathrm{KL}\ge0\).

  • Markov: for \(X\ge0\), \(P(X\ge t)\le\mathbb EX/t\). Chebyshev: \(P(|X-\mu|\ge t)\le\sigma^2/t^2\). Both hold for any distribution, so they are loose for any particular one (the output compares them with the exponential’s exact tail).

  • Moment generating function \(M(s)=\mathbb E[e^{sX}]\): \(M^{(k)}(0)=\mathbb E[X^k]\). The MGF of a sum of independent variables is the product of their MGFs.

  • Shape: skewness \(\mathbb E[Z^3]\) (asymmetry) and excess kurtosis \(\mathbb E[Z^4]-3\) (heavy tails) of the standardised \(Z\). Laplace and exponential noise have the same mean and variance as a normal but very different tails.

"""Probability: expectation, variance, Jensen, tail bounds, moment generating functions."""
import math

import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
N = 2_000_000

X, Y = rng.exponential(2.0, N), rng.uniform(0, 6, N)          # independent; E 2, 3; Var 4, 3
print(f"[linear] E[3X + 2Y + 1] = {np.mean(3 * X + 2 * Y + 1):.3f}   formula 3*2 + 2*3 + 1 = 13")
print(f"[var] Var(X + Y) = {np.var(X + Y):.3f} = Var X + Var Y = {4 + 3}   Var(3X) = {np.var(3 * X):.2f} = 9 Var X = 36")
print(f"[var] Var X = E[X^2] - (E X)^2: {np.mean(X**2) - np.mean(X)**2:.3f}")

G = rng.gamma(3.0, 1.0, N)
print(f"[jensen] log is concave: E[log G] = {np.mean(np.log(G)):.4f} <= log E[G] = {np.log(np.mean(G)):.4f}")

# Tail of Exponential(mean 1): exact e^{-t}; Markov 1/t; Chebyshev (one-sided) <= 1/(t-1)^2.
print("[tail] t   exact P(X >= t)   Markov 1/t   Chebyshev 1/(t-1)^2")
for t in (2, 4, 8):
    print(f"[tail] {t}   {math.exp(-t):.5f}           {1 / t:.4f}       {1 / (t - 1) ** 2:.4f}")

# MGF of Poisson(lam): M(s) = exp(lam (e^s - 1)). M'(0) = E X, M''(0) = E X^2.
lam, h = 3.0, 1e-4
M = lambda s: math.exp(lam * (math.exp(s) - 1))
m1, m2 = (M(h) - M(-h)) / (2 * h), (M(h) - 2 * M(0) + M(-h)) / h**2
P = rng.poisson(lam, N)
print(f"[mgf] M'(0) = {m1:.4f} (E X = {P.mean():.4f})   M''(0) = {m2:.4f} (E X^2 = {np.mean(P.astype(float)**2):.4f})")

# Skewness and kurtosis distinguish shapes that share a mean and variance.
for name, s in (("normal", rng.normal(0, 1, N)), ("laplace", rng.laplace(0, 1 / math.sqrt(2), N)),
                ("exponential-1", rng.exponential(1, N) - 1)):
    z = (s - s.mean()) / s.std()
    print(f"[shape] {name:13s} mean {s.mean():+.3f} var {s.var():.3f} skew {np.mean(z**3):+.3f} excess kurt {np.mean(z**4) - 3:+.3f}")
