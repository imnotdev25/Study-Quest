A fair comparison fixes the model weights and precision, the hardware, the workload (lengths, arrival process, shared prefixes), the sampling parameters, and the SLO; reports goodput at the SLO plus the latency curve; tunes each engine’s main knobs equally; and checks that outputs agree (same greedy tokens, or the same eval score). On Evaluating Performance of LLM Inference Serving Systems documents how often published comparisons break one of these rules, for example by quoting TPOT without ITL tails or by sweeping only closed-loop load.
