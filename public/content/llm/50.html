🚀 Starting point

T5 (Raffel et al., 2019) casts every task as text in, text out (“translate English to German: …”). The encoder reads the input with bidirectional attention. The decoder generates with causal self-attention plus cross-attention, whose queries come from the decoder and keys and values from the encoder’s output.

T5: the decoder’s queries attend to the encoder’s output through cross-attention.

✏️ Derivation: cross-attention, span corruption and relative position bias

Step 1. Cross-attention: \(Q=Y_{\text{dec}}W_Q\), \(K=H_{\text{enc}}W_K\), \(V=H_{\text{enc}}W_V\), \(O=\operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt d)V\). No causal mask here: the whole input is known.

Step 2. Span corruption: replace random spans (15% of tokens, mean length 3) with sentinels \(\langle X\rangle,\langle Y\rangle\), and train the decoder to output only the missing spans: “\(\langle X\rangle\) for inviting \(\langle Y\rangle\) last”. Targets are short, so training is cheap.

Step 3. Loss: next-token NLL on the decoder output, conditioned on the corrupted input: \(-\sum_t\log p(y_t\mid y_{<t},\tilde x)\).

Step 4. Relative position bias: add a learned scalar \(b_{h}(\operatorname{bucket}(j-i))\) to each attention logit: \(S_{ij}=q_i^\top k_j/\sqrt d+b_h(j-i)\). Buckets are exact for small offsets and logarithmic for large ones, so the model generalizes somewhat beyond trained lengths.

Step 5. Cost: encoder–decoder with \(L\) layers each has about the parameters of a \(2L\)-layer decoder, but the input is processed once and not re-attended causally.
