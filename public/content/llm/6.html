🚀 Starting point

Starting point. Unconstrained: minimise \(f(x)\) over all \(x\). At a minimum the gradient is zero and the Hessian is positive semi-definite. Constrained: minimise \(f(x)\) subject to \(g(x)=0\) or \(h(x)\le0\). At a constrained minimum the gradient need not vanish; it only has to point straight out of the feasible set. Real-life version: the lowest point of a valley is where the ground is flat. The lowest point you can reach while staying on a hiking trail is where the trail runs along a contour line, so the downhill direction is perpendicular to the trail.

Unconstrained: conditions and gradient descent

  • First order: \(\nabla f(x^\star)=0\). Second order: \(H(x^\star)\succeq0\) (all eigenvalues \(\ge0\)) for a minimum; mixed signs mean a saddle. For \(f=x^3-3x+y^2\), \((1,0)\) has eigenvalues \(\{6,2\}\) (minimum) and \((-1,0)\) has \(\{-6,2\}\) (saddle).

  • Convexity: if \(H\succeq0\) everywhere, every stationary point is a global minimum. Cross-entropy in the logits is convex (its Hessian is the softmax Jacobian); a neural network’s loss in its weights is not.

✏️ Derivation: Gradient descent on a quadratic, and the condition number

Step 1. \(f(x)=\tfrac12x^\top Ax-b^\top x\), \(A\) symmetric with eigenvalues \(\mu=\lambda_{\min}\le\dots\le\lambda_{\max}=L\). The minimiser is \(x^\star=A^{-1}b\).

Step 2. One step \(x\leftarrow x-\eta(Ax-b)\) gives \(x-x^\star\leftarrow(I-\eta A)(x-x^\star)\).

Step 3. In the eigenbasis each coordinate shrinks by \(|1-\eta\lambda_i|\). With \(\eta=1/L\), the slowest is \(1-\mu/L=1-1/\kappa\).

Step 4. Reaching error \(\epsilon\) takes \(k\approx\kappa\ln(1/\epsilon)\) steps. The code matches this to within 1%: 130, 1375 and 13,809 steps at \(\kappa=10,100,1000\). Newton’s method, \(x\leftarrow x-H^{-1}\nabla f\), rescales every direction by its curvature and solves a quadratic in one step. Adam’s per-coordinate scaling is a cheap diagonal approximation of that idea.

Equality constraints: Lagrange multipliers

\(f=(x_1-2)^2+(x_2-2)^2\) on the line \(x_1+x_2=1\). At \(x^\star\) the line is tangent to a contour, so \(\nabla f\) and \(\nabla g\) are parallel.

✏️ Derivation: The Lagrange condition

Step 1. Move along the constraint: any feasible direction \(d\) has \(\nabla g^\top d=0\). At a constrained minimum \(f\) can’t decrease to first order along such \(d\), so \(\nabla f^\top d=0\) for every \(d\perp\nabla g\).

Step 2. A vector orthogonal to everything orthogonal to \(\nabla g\) is a multiple of it: \(\nabla f=-\lambda\nabla g\).

Step 3. Equivalently, \(x^\star\) is a stationary point of the Lagrangian \(\mathcal L(x,\lambda)=f(x)+\lambda g(x)\): \(\partial_x\mathcal L=0\) gives step 2 and \(\partial_\lambda\mathcal L=0\) gives the constraint back.

✏️ Derivation: Softmax is the maximum-entropy distribution

Step 1. Maximise \(H(p)=-\sum_ip_i\log p_i\) subject to \(\sum_ip_i=1\) and \(\sum_ip_iE_i=\mu\).

Step 2. \(\mathcal L=-\sum p_i\log p_i+\alpha(\sum p_i-1)-\lambda(\sum p_iE_i-\mu)\). \(\partial/\partial p_i\): \(-\log p_i-1+\alpha-\lambda E_i=0\).

Step 3. So \(p_i\propto e^{-\lambda E_i}\), normalised: \(\boxed{p_i=e^{-\lambda E_i}/\sum_je^{-\lambda E_j}}\), the Gibbs distribution, which is softmax of the logits \(-\lambda E\). \(\lambda\) is fixed by the mean constraint (the code finds \(\lambda=0.42\) by bisection and checks that every feasible perturbation lowers the entropy). Temperature is \(1/\lambda\).

✏️ Derivation: The optimal RLHF policy

Step 1. Maximise \(J(\pi)=\sum_y\pi(y)r(y)-\beta\sum_y\pi(y)\log\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y)}\) subject to \(\sum_y\pi(y)=1\).

Step 2. Stationarity: \(r(y)-\beta\big(\log\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y)}+1\big)+\alpha=0\), so \(\log\pi(y)=\log\pi_{\text{ref}}(y)+r(y)/\beta+\text{const}\).

Step 3. \(\boxed{\pi^\star(y)=\pi_{\text{ref}}(y)e^{r(y)/\beta}/Z}\), and plugging back in, \(J(\pi^\star)=\beta\log Z\). Solving step 3 for \(r\) gives \(r=\beta\log\frac{\pi^\star}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}+\beta\log Z\), the substitution that turns RLHF into DPO (Session 26 onward).

Inequality constraints: KKT

For \(\min f(x)\) subject to \(h_k(x)\le0\), the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions at a (regular) optimum are: stationarity \(\nabla f+\sum_k\mu_k\nabla h_k=0\); primal feasibility \(h_k\le0\); dual feasibility \(\mu_k\ge0\); and complementary slackness \(\mu_kh_k=0\), meaning each constraint is either active (\(h_k=0\)) or has zero multiplier. Example: \(\min\lVert x-c\rVert^2\) s.t. \(x\ge0\) gives \(x=\max(c,0)\), the ReLU, with \(\mu_k=2\max(-c_k,0)\). Projected gradient descent (step, then clip to the feasible set) reaches it. PPO’s clipped objective and trust regions are this kind of problem, solved approximately.

"""Math I: unconstrained and constrained optimisation, with the two constrained problems behind
softmax (max entropy) and RLHF (KL-regularised reward maximisation)."""
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

# 1. Gradient descent on f(x) = 1/2 x^T A x - b^T x. Speed is set by the condition number kappa.
print("[gd] kappa   GD steps to 1e-6   theory ~ kappa*ln(1e6)   Newton steps")
for kappa in (10, 100, 1000):
    A = np.diag(np.linspace(1, kappa, 10)); b = np.ones(10); x_star = np.linalg.solve(A, b)
    x = np.zeros(10)
    for k in range(1, 100_000):
        x -= (A @ x - b) / kappa                          # step 1/L, L = largest eigenvalue
        if np.linalg.norm(x - x_star) < 1e-6 * np.linalg.norm(x_star):
            break
    xn = np.zeros(10) - np.linalg.solve(A, A @ np.zeros(10) - b)   # one Newton step
    print(f"[gd] {kappa:5d}   {k:8d}            {kappa * np.log(1e6):8.0f}              "
          f"{1 if np.allclose(xn, x_star) else '>1'}")

# 2. Second-derivative test: f = x^3 - 3x + y^2 has critical points (1, 0) and (-1, 0).
for pt in ((1.0, 0.0), (-1.0, 0.0)):
    ev = np.linalg.eigvalsh(np.diag([6 * pt[0], 2.0]))
    kind = "minimum" if (ev > 0).all() else "saddle" if (ev < 0).any() and (ev > 0).any() else "maximum"
    print(f"[hessian] critical point {pt}: Hessian eigenvalues {ev} -> {kind}")

# 3. Max entropy subject to sum p = 1 and E_p[energy] = mu  ->  Gibbs / softmax p ~ exp(-lam * energy).
E, mu = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0]), 1.0
gibbs = lambda lam: np.exp(-lam * E) / np.exp(-lam * E).sum()
lo, hi = -20.0, 20.0
for _ in range(100):                                       # mean energy decreases in lam: bisect
    mid = (lo + hi) / 2
    lo, hi = (mid, hi) if gibbs(mid) @ E > mu else (lo, mid)
p = gibbs(lo)
H = lambda q: -(q * np.log(q)).sum()
null = np.linalg.svd(np.vstack([np.ones(4), E]))[2][2:]   # directions keeping both constraints
worse = all(H(p + 0.01 * (rng.normal(size=2) @ null)) < H(p) for _ in range(1000))
print(f"[maxent] lambda = {lo:.4f}, p = {np.round(p, 4)}, mean energy {p @ E:.4f}, entropy {H(p):.4f}")
print(f"[maxent] 1000 random feasible perturbations all have lower entropy: {worse}")

# 4. RLHF objective: max_pi E_pi[r] - beta KL(pi || pi_ref)  ->  pi* ~ pi_ref exp(r / beta).
K, beta = 6, 0.5
pi_ref, r = rng.dirichlet(np.ones(K)), rng.normal(size=K)
J = lambda pi: pi @ r - beta * (pi * np.log(pi / pi_ref)).sum()
pi_star = pi_ref * np.exp(r / beta); pi_star /= pi_star.sum()
best_random = max(J(rng.dirichlet(np.ones(K))) for _ in range(20_000))
print(f"[rlhf] J(pi*) = {J(pi_star):.4f} = beta log Z = {beta * np.log((pi_ref * np.exp(r / beta)).sum()):.4f};"
      f" best of 20,000 random policies {best_random:.4f}")

# 5. KKT: min |x - c|^2 s.t. x >= 0. Solution max(c, 0); multipliers mu = 2 max(-c, 0) >= 0, mu * x = 0.
c = np.array([1.5, -0.7, 0.2, -2.0])
x = np.zeros(4)
for _ in range(200):
    x = np.maximum(x - 0.1 * 2 * (x - c), 0)                 # projected gradient descent
mu_kkt = 2 * (x - c)                                         # stationarity: 2(x - c) - mu = 0
print(f"[kkt] projected GD x = {np.round(x, 4)} (= max(c,0)), multipliers {np.round(mu_kkt, 4) + 0.0}, "
      f"complementary slackness mu*x = {np.round(mu_kkt * x, 6) + 0.0}")

🔑 Key ideas

  • Softmax and the RLHF optimum are both answers to constrained optimisation problems; the Lagrange multiplier is the temperature in one and \(1/\beta\) in the other.

  • No random policy among 20,000 beats \(\pi^\star\), and \(J(\pi^\star)=\beta\log Z\) to four decimals.

  • Ill-conditioning (large \(\kappa\)) is why plain gradient descent is slow, and why adaptive optimisers and normalisation layers help.
