🚀 Starting point

Starting point. A joint distribution \(p(x,y)\) (or density \(f(x,y)\)) describes two variables together. From it: marginals by summing out the other variable, \(p(x)=\sum_yp(x,y)\); conditionals by dividing, \(p(y\mid x)=p(x,y)/p(x)\); and independence when \(p(x,y)=p(x)p(y)\) everywhere.

Two discrete variables

The code’s table has \(X\in\{0,1\}\) in rows and \(Y\in\{0,1,2\}\) in columns. Its rows don’t share a shape (\(P(Y\mid X=1)\) puts most mass on 2, \(P(Y\mid X=0)\) on 0), so the variables are dependent. Mutual information \(I(X;Y)=\mathrm{KL}\big(p(x,y)\,\Vert\,p(x)p(y)\big)=H(Y)-H(Y\mid X)\) measures how far from independent they are, in nats: how much knowing \(X\) reduces uncertainty about \(Y\). It is zero exactly when they are independent.

Two continuous variables

✏️ Derivation: \(f(x,y)=x+y\) on the unit square

Step 1. Valid density: \(\int_0^1\!\int_0^1(x+y)\,dy\,dx=\int_0^1(x+\tfrac12)dx=1\).

Step 2. Marginal: \(f_X(x)=\int_0^1(x+y)dy=x+\tfrac12\). Conditional: \(f(y\mid x)=\frac{x+y}{x+1/2}\), which depends on \(x\): dependent.

Step 3. \(\mathbb EX=\int_0^1x(x+\tfrac12)dx=\tfrac13+\tfrac14=\tfrac7{12}\), and by symmetry \(\mathbb EY=\tfrac7{12}\).

Step 4. \(\mathbb E[XY]=\iint xy(x+y)=\tfrac16+\tfrac16=\tfrac13\). \(\operatorname{Cov}=\tfrac13-\tfrac{49}{144}=-\tfrac1{144}\). Slightly negative: high \(x\) makes high \(y\) a little less necessary to reach high density.

Moments of joint distributions

✏️ Derivation: Iterated expectation and the law of total variance

Step 1. \(\mathbb E[X]=\mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[X\mid K]\big]\): average the conditional means, weighted by \(P(K)\).

Step 2. \(\operatorname{Var}X=\mathbb E[X^2]-(\mathbb EX)^2=\mathbb E\big[\operatorname{Var}(X\mid K)+\mathbb E[X\mid K]^2\big]-\mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[X\mid K]\big]^2\).

Step 3. \(\boxed{\operatorname{Var}X=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(X\mid K)]+\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[X\mid K])}\): noise within groups plus spread between group means. For the mixture in the code, \(0.475+5.25\): almost all the variance comes from which component you are in. In RL, \(K\) is the prompt, and GRPO’s group baseline removes the between-prompt term.

"""Joint RVs: two discrete, two continuous, moments of joint distributions, covariance and correlation."""
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

# 1. Two discrete variables: a joint table, marginals, conditionals, independence, mutual information.
P = np.array([[0.20, 0.10, 0.05],
              [0.05, 0.25, 0.35]])                             # rows X in {0,1}, columns Y in {0,1,2}
px, py = P.sum(1), P.sum(0)
print(f"[discrete] P(X) = {px}   P(Y) = {py}   P(Y | X=1) = {np.round(P[1] / px[1], 3)}")
print(f"[discrete] independent? max |P(x,y) - P(x)P(y)| = {np.abs(P - np.outer(px, py)).max():.3f}")
MI = (P * np.log(P / np.outer(px, py))).sum()
print(f"[discrete] mutual information I(X;Y) = KL(P_xy || P_x P_y) = {MI:.4f} nats")
x_vals, y_vals = np.array([0, 1]), np.array([0, 1, 2])
EX, EY = px @ x_vals, py @ y_vals
EXY = (P * np.outer(x_vals, y_vals)).sum()
print(f"[discrete] E X {EX:.2f}  E Y {EY:.2f}  E XY {EXY:.2f}  Cov = E XY - E X E Y = {EXY - EX * EY:.4f}")

# 2. Two continuous variables: f(x, y) = x + y on the unit square.
#    Marginal f_X(x) = x + 1/2;  E X = 7/12;  E XY = 1/3;  Cov = 1/3 - 49/144 = -1/144.
n = 600
c = (np.arange(n) + 0.5) / n
X, Y = np.meshgrid(c, c, indexing="ij")
f = X + Y
dA = 1 / n**2
print(f"[continuous] total mass {f.sum() * dA:.6f}   E X {(X * f).sum() * dA:.6f} (7/12 = {7 / 12:.6f})")
print(f"[continuous] Cov(X,Y) {(X * Y * f).sum() * dA - ((X * f).sum() * dA) ** 2:.6f}  (-1/144 = {-1 / 144:.6f})")
u = rng.random((3_000_000, 3))
keep = u[:, 2] * 2 < u[:, 0] + u[:, 1]                         # rejection sampling from f (max 2)
s = u[keep, :2]
print(f"[continuous] rejection-sampled {len(s):,} points: Cov {np.cov(s.T)[0, 1]:.6f}")

# 3. Iterated expectation and total variance: X | K ~ N(mu_K, s_K^2), K in {0, 1} w.p. {0.3, 0.7}.
w, mu, sd = np.array([0.3, 0.7]), np.array([-2.0, 3.0]), np.array([1.0, 0.5])
K = rng.random(2_000_000) < w[1]
Xm = np.where(K, rng.normal(mu[1], sd[1], K.size), rng.normal(mu[0], sd[0], K.size))
EX = w @ mu
VarX = w @ sd**2 + w @ (mu - EX) ** 2                          # E[Var(X|K)] + Var(E[X|K])
print(f"[total] E X = E[E[X|K]] = {EX:.3f} (sim {Xm.mean():.3f});  Var X = {w @ sd**2:.3f} + {w @ (mu - EX) ** 2:.3f}"
      f" = {VarX:.3f} (sim {Xm.var():.3f})")

# 4. Correlation: scale-free, bounded by 1, and blind to nonlinear dependence.
Z = rng.normal(size=1_000_000)
W = 0.6 * Z + 0.8 * rng.normal(size=Z.size)
print(f"[corr] corr(Z, W) {np.corrcoef(Z, W)[0, 1]:.4f}   corr(5Z + 2, -3W) {np.corrcoef(5 * Z + 2, -3 * W)[0, 1]:.4f}")
print(f"[corr] Y = Z^2: corr(Z, Y) {np.corrcoef(Z, Z**2)[0, 1]:+.4f} but E[Y | |Z|>2] = {np.mean(Z[np.abs(Z) > 2] ** 2):.2f}"
      f" vs E[Y] = {np.mean(Z**2):.2f}  (uncorrelated, not independent)")
