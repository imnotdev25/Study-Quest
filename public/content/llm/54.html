🚀 Starting point

An MoE layer replaces one MLP with \(E\) expert MLPs and a router. For each token, the router picks the top-\(k\) experts and mixes their outputs. Total parameters grow with \(E\), but compute per token grows only with \(k\). Mixtral 8\(\times\)7B: 46.7B total, 12.9B active (\(k=2\) of 8).

A top-2 MoE layer with one shared expert. The token pays for 3 expert MLPs but the layer stores 9. DeepSeek-V3 does this with 256 routed experts (top-8); Kimi K3 with 896 (top-16).

✏️ Derivation: routing, and why it needs balancing

Step 1. Router logits \(r=xW_r\in\mathbb R^E\), gates \(g=\operatorname{softmax}(r)\). Output \(y=\sum_{e\in\operatorname{TopK}(g)}\tilde g_e\,\operatorname{MLP}_e(x)\), with \(\tilde g\) the top-\(k\) gates renormalized to sum to 1.

Step 2. The gradient reaches \(W_r\) only through the chosen experts’ gates, so experts that win early get trained, get better, and win more: routing collapse.

Step 3. Switch auxiliary loss: with \(f_e\) the fraction of tokens sent to expert \(e\) and \(P_e\) the mean gate probability for \(e\): \(L_{\text{aux}}=E\sum_ef_eP_e\).

Step 4. Uniform routing: \(f_e=P_e=1/E\Rightarrow L_{\text{aux}}=E\cdot E\cdot\frac{1}{E^2}=1\). Full collapse onto one expert: \(f=P=1\) for it \(\Rightarrow L_{\text{aux}}=E\). (\(f\) isn’t differentiable, so the gradient flows through \(P\).)

Step 5. Capacity: each expert processes at most \(\text{cf}\cdot kT/E\) tokens per batch, and overflow tokens skip the layer (via the residual).

✏️ Derivation: DeepSeekMoE: fine-grained and shared experts, balancing without a loss

Step 1. Fine-grained: split each expert into \(m\) smaller ones and route to \(mk\). Same compute, but \(\binom{mE}{mk}\gg\binom Ek\) possible combinations, so specialization is finer.

Step 2. Shared experts: \(y=\sum_{s}\operatorname{MLP}^{\text{shared}}_s(x)+\sum_{e\in\operatorname{TopK}}g_e\operatorname{MLP}_e(x)\). Common knowledge lives in the shared part, so routed experts don’t all relearn it.

Step 3. Auxiliary-loss-free balancing (DeepSeek-V3): add a bias \(b_e\) to the scores only for choosing: \(\operatorname{TopK}(s_e+b_e)\), but mix with the unbiased \(s_e\). After each step, \(b_e\gets b_e-\gamma\operatorname{sign}(\text{load}_e-\overline{\text{load}})\).

Step 4. An overloaded expert’s bias falls until it’s chosen less. Balance is achieved without a loss term pulling the router away from what’s best for the language-model loss.

Step 5. Later variants change the score function: V3 uses \(\sigma(\cdot)\), V4 uses \(\sqrt{\softplus(\cdot)}\) (from its config.json: scoring_func: sqrtsoftplus), and Kimi K3 sets the bias from a quantile of score margins (Quantile Balancing).

⚙️ Algorithm: top-\(k\) MoE layer with bias-based balancing
Input: tokens \(X\), router \(W_r\), experts \(\{\operatorname{MLP}_e\}\), shared experts, bias \(b\), rate \(\gamma\)
\(s\gets\sigma(XW_r)\) ▷ affinity per token and expert
\(\mathcal T\gets\operatorname{TopK}(s+b)\) per token ▷ bias affects choice only
\(g\gets s_{\mathcal T}/\sum s_{\mathcal T}\)
\(Y\gets\sum_s\operatorname{MLP}^{\text{sh}}_s(X)+\sum_{e\in\mathcal T}g_e\operatorname{MLP}_e(X)\) ▷ dispatch tokens to experts
\(b_e\gets b_e-\gamma\operatorname{sign}(\text{load}_e-\overline{\text{load}})\) ▷ outside autograd
