🚀 Starting point

GPT is a decoder-only Transformer trained with the next-token loss (Session 11). GPT-1 (2018, 117M) fine-tuned per task. GPT-2 (2019, 1.5B) moved LayerNorm to the start of each block (pre-norm, Session 10) and showed zero-shot transfer. GPT-3 (2020, 175B, 96 layers, \(D=12288\), 96 heads) showed in-context learning: put examples in the prompt and the model continues the pattern, with no gradient update.

✏️ Derivation: power-law scaling (Kaplan et al., 2020)

Step 1. Empirically, test loss falls as a power law in parameters \(N\) (with enough data): \(L(N)\approx(N_c/N)^{\alpha_N}\), with \(\alpha_N\approx0.076\).

Step 2. Take logs: \(\log L=\alpha_N\log N_c-\alpha_N\log N\). A power law is a straight line on a log–log plot.

Step 3. Doubling \(N\) multiplies \(L\) by \(2^{-0.076}=0.949\): about 5% lower loss per doubling, forever, until something else (data, compute) limits it.

Step 4. Training compute \(C\approx6ND\) FLOPs (Session 10: about 2 FLOPs per parameter per token forward, 4 backward). This identity is what lets us trade \(N\) against \(D\), which is the next chapter’s question.

Step 5. Kaplan’s fits suggested putting most extra compute into \(N\). Chinchilla later found that was wrong because the runs weren’t trained to convergence with matched learning-rate schedules.

Kaplan’s fit \(L=(N_c/N)^{0.076}\) with \(N_c=8.8\times10^{13}\): a straight line on log–log axes.

🔑 Key ideas

  • Decoder-only + next-token loss: every position is a training example.

  • Loss falls as a power law in size, which made “just scale it” a rational plan.
