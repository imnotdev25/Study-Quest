🚀 Starting point

RoPE pair \(i\) rotates by \(\theta_i=p\cdot\omega_i\) with \(\omega_i=\text{base}^{-2i/d}\) (Session 24). Its wavelength \(\lambda_i=2\pi/\omega_i\) is the number of tokens for one full turn. High-frequency pairs encode local order. Low-frequency pairs encode long-range position, but if the model never saw a full turn during training, positions beyond training length are unfamiliar.

Each RoPE pair rotates at its own speed. YaRN treats pairs differently depending on which side of the training length their wavelength falls.

✏️ Derivation: position interpolation, NTK scaling and YaRN

Step 1. Longest wavelength (64 rotary dims, last pair \(i=31\)): \(\lambda_{\max}=2\pi\,\text{base}^{62/64}\). Base \(10^4\): 47,117 tokens. Base \(5\times10^5\) (Llama 3): about 2.08M. A bigger base slows every low-frequency pair.

Step 2. Position interpolation: to extend by \(s\times\), use \(p/s\). All rotations stay within the trained range, but high-frequency pairs get \(s\times\) coarser, which hurts local order.

Step 3. NTK-aware: instead raise the base, \(\text{base}'=\text{base}\cdot s^{d/(d-2)}\). Highest frequencies barely change, and the lowest is stretched by about \(s\).

Step 4. YaRN: per pair, compare its wavelength with the training length \(L\). Pairs with \(\lambda_i\ll L\) are left alone, pairs with \(\lambda_i>L\) are interpolated by \(s\), and pairs in between are blended. It also scales logits by about \(0.1\ln s+1\) to keep attention entropy steady. DeepSeek-V4 uses YaRN with factor 16 from 64K to 1M (its config).

Step 5. NoPE layers: some layers use no positional encoding at all, and causal masking alone leaks enough order information. Llama 4 interleaves NoPE layers (“iRoPE”), and Kimi K3’s MLA layers are NoPE, with order carried by the linear-attention layers.
