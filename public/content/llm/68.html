🚀 Starting point

A shared backbone feeds both the actor head and the critic head, so its parameters receive \(g=g_\pi+c_Vg_V\). If the two gradients point in different directions, one loss’s progress costs the other’s. The cosine similarity \(\cos(g_\pi,g_V)\) measures how aligned they are.

Total: 20 minutes

1. Retrieval + concept refresh — 5 minutes

Quick retrieval:

  1. What sign should the entropy term have in a loss that is minimized?

  2. Why is the entropy gradient zero at an exactly uniform categorical distribution?

An actor–critic model often has:

\[X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T\times D_{\text{in}}}\]

\[H=f_{\theta_s}(X) \in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T\times D}\]

with two heads:

\[Z=W_\pi H \in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T\times A}\]

\[V=W_VH \in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T}\]

The backbone parameters \(\theta_s\) receive both actor and critic gradients:

\[g_\pi=\nabla_{\theta_s}L_\pi, \qquad g_V=\nabla_{\theta_s}L_V\]

For:

\[L=L_\pi+c_VL_V\]

the shared gradient is:

\[g=g_\pi+c_Vg_V\]

Their relationship can be inspected with cosine similarity:

\[\cos(g_\pi,g_V) = \frac{g_\pi^\top g_V} {\lVert g_\pi\rVert\lVert g_V\rVert}\]

  • Positive cosine: locally compatible updates.

  • Near zero: mostly independent directions.

  • Negative cosine: gradient interference.

  • Large \(c_V\lVert g_V\rVert\): critic may dominate even if its raw loss looks reasonable.

For LLM PPO, the shared network is usually the transformer, with an LM policy head and scalar value head. Per-loss gradient measurement requires retained graphs or extra backward passes, so it is usually an occasional diagnostic rather than an every-step metric.

✏️ Derivation: when the critic undoes the actor

Step 1. One step on the backbone: \(\Delta\theta=-\eta(g_\pi+c_Vg_V)\).

Step 2. First-order change in the actor loss: \(\Delta L_\pi\approx g_\pi^\top\Delta\theta=-\eta\big(\|g_\pi\|^2+c_V\,g_\pi^\top g_V\big)\).

Step 3. The actor loss increases if \(c_Vg_\pi^\top g_V<-\|g_\pi\|^2\), i.e. \(\cos(g_\pi,g_V)<-\frac{\|g_\pi\|}{c_V\|g_V\|}\).

Step 4. So conflict needs both an opposing direction and a critic gradient larger than \(\|g_\pi\|/c_V\). Since \(L_V\) is in squared return units, a large reward scale inflates \(\|g_V\|\) and makes this easy.

Step 5. Example: \(g_\pi=(1,0)\), \(g_V=(-3,1)\), \(c_V=0.5\): \(g=(-0.5,0.5)\), and \(g^\top g_\pi=-0.5<0\). With \(c_V=0.25\): \(g=(0.25,0.25)\), \(g^\top g_\pi=0.25>0\).

⚙️ Algorithm: measure actor–critic gradient interference
Input: shared parameters \(\theta_s\), losses \(L_\pi,L_V\)
\(g_\pi\gets\nabla_{\theta_s}L_\pi\) (retain graph); \(g_V\gets\nabla_{\theta_s}L_V\)
log \(\|g_\pi\|\), \(c_V\|g_V\|\), \(\cos(g_\pi,g_V)\), and \(g_\pi^\top(g_\pi+c_Vg_V)\)
if the last value is negative for many steps then lower \(c_V\), normalize returns, or separate the heads’ trunks
end

values = value_head(hidden).squeeze(-1)

log_probs = F.log_softmax(

For a small gradient-descent step using \(g_V\), the first-order actor-loss change is:

\[\Delta L_\pi \approx -\eta g_\pi^\top g_V\]

If their cosine is negative:

\[g_\pi^\top g_V<0\]

then:

\[\Delta L_\pi>0\]

so the critic step locally increases the actor loss.

As \(c_V\) grows:

\[g_\pi+c_Vg_V\]

becomes increasingly aligned with \(g_V\). Its limiting direction is the critic-gradient direction, assuming \(g_V\neq0\).

Check answers:

  1. The shared backbone. Each task-specific head normally receives only its corresponding loss gradient.

  2. The gradients point in exactly opposite directions; improving one objective locally worsens the other along that direction.

  3. They cancel, producing a zero shared-backbone gradient even though both individual gradients are nonzero.

  4. Compare actor and critic gradient norms and their cosine similarity on the shared backbone. Also inspect whether value_coef makes the weighted critic norm dominate.

  5. Separate gradient extraction retains or recomputes the graph, increasing memory and computation. Sampled diagnostic batches are usually sufficient.

Retrieval answers:

  • A minimized PPO objective uses: \[-c_HH\] to encourage entropy.

  • Uniform probabilities maximize entropy, so the entropy gradient is zero at that point.
