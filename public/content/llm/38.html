🚀 Starting point

Softmax cross-entropy is the categorical NLL from Part 0: \(\ell=-\log p_y\) with \(p=\operatorname{softmax}(z)\). Here we derive its gradient the long way, through the full softmax Jacobian, to see why the shortcut \(p-\onehot(y)\) is exact.

Total: 20 minutes

1. Concept refresh — 5 minutes

For hidden representations and an output projection:

\[H\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times D},\qquad W\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times C}\]

\[Z=HW\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times C}\]

For example \(i\), softmax converts logits into probabilities:

\[p_{ic}=\frac{e^{z_{ic}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{C}e^{z_{ij}}}\]

Cross-entropy for target class \(y_i\) is:

\[\ell_i=-\log p_{i,y_i}\]

Equivalently:

\[\ell_i = -z_{i,y_i} + \log\sum_{c=1}^{C}e^{z_{ic}}\]

With mean reduction:

\[L=\frac{1}{B}\sum_i\ell_i\]

The important combined derivative is:

\[\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_{ic}} = \frac{p_{ic}-\mathbf{1}[c=y_i]}{B}\]

In matrix form:

\[\frac{\partial L}{\partial Z} = \frac{P-\operatorname{onehot}(y)}{B}\]

Then ordinary matrix-multiplication backprop gives:

\[\frac{\partial L}{\partial W} = H^\top\frac{\partial L}{\partial Z} \in\mathbb{R}^{D\times C}\]

\[\frac{\partial L}{\partial H} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial Z}W^\top \in\mathbb{R}^{B\times D}\]

For language models, logits are [B,T,V]. Conceptually, cross-entropy treats them as [B*T,V], excluding ignored prompt or padding positions.

For numerical stability, compute:

\[\log\sum_c e^{z_c} = m+\log\sum_c e^{z_c-m}, \qquad m=\max_c z_c\]

✏️ Derivation: through the softmax Jacobian

Step 1. Softmax Jacobian: \(\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}=\frac{\delta_{ij}e^{z_i}\Sigma-e^{z_i}e^{z_j}}{\Sigma^2}=p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j)\), with \(\Sigma=\sum_ke^{z_k}\). As a matrix: \(J=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top\).

Step 2. Loss in terms of \(p\): \(\ell=-\log p_y\Rightarrow\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial p_i}=-\frac{\mathbb 1[i=y]}{p_y}\).

Step 3. Chain rule: \(\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial z_j}=\sum_i\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}=-\frac{1}{p_y}\,p_y(\delta_{yj}-p_j)=p_j-\delta_{yj}\).

Step 4. So \(\nabla_z\ell=p-\onehot(y)\). Its entries sum to \(1-1=0\), because adding a constant to all logits doesn’t change the loss.

Step 5. Batch with mean reduction, \(Z=HW\): \(\Delta=\frac{P-Y}{B}\), \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial W}=H^\top\Delta\), \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial H}=\Delta W^\top\).

Step 6. Why fuse: computing \(p\) and then \(\log p\) separately loses precision when \(p_y\) is tiny. The fused form \(\LSE(z)-z_y\) never takes the log of a small number.

⚙️ Algorithm: manual softmax cross-entropy backward
Input: \(H\in\mathbb R^{B\times D}\), \(W\in\mathbb R^{D\times C}\), labels \(y\)
\(Z\gets HW\); \(P\gets\operatorname{softmax}(Z-\max Z)\)
\(L\gets\frac1B\sum_i\big(\LSE(Z_i)-Z_{i,y_i}\big)\)
\(\Delta\gets(P-\onehot(y))/B\)
\(\nabla_W\gets H^\top\Delta\); \(\nabla_H\gets\Delta W^\top\)

3. Checks for understanding — 5 minutes

  1. Why does the correct-class logit receive gradient \((p_y-1)/B\), while other logits receive \(p_c/B\)?

  2. Why does every row of \(\partial L/\partial Z\) sum to zero?

  3. Prediction: if the model assigns probability \(1\) to the correct class, what happens to the logits gradient?

  4. Debugging: training loss is nan only when logits become very large. What unstable implementation is likely being used?

  5. For LM logits [B,T,V] with some labels equal to ignore_index, what should replace \(B\) in the mean-gradient denominator?

Solution — open after attempting

logits = H @ W

z = logits.detach()

row_max = z.max(dim=-1, keepdim=True).values
shifted = z - row_max

log_normalizer = (
    shifted.exp()
    .sum(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    .log()
)

log_probs = shifted - log_normalizer

manual_loss = -log_probs[
    torch.arange(B), targets
].mean()

probs = log_probs.exp()

manual_dlogits = probs.clone()
manual_dlogits[torch.arange(B), targets] -= 1
manual_dlogits /= B

manual_dW = H.detach().T @ manual_dlogits
manual_dH = manual_dlogits @ W.detach().T

The gradient rows sum to zero:

\[\sum_c(p_c-\mathbf 1[c=y]) = \sum_c p_c-1 = 0\]

Softmax is invariant to adding the same constant to every logit:

\[\operatorname{softmax}(z+k)=\operatorname{softmax}(z)\]

The naïve implementation overflows because \(e^{1000}\) becomes infinity. Its leading probability becomes inf / inf, producing nan. Subtracting the row maximum avoids this without changing the probabilities.

Check answers:

  1. Increasing the correct logit lowers the loss, producing \(p_y-1\leq0\). Increasing an incorrect logit raises its probability and therefore the loss, producing \(p_c\geq0\).

  2. Softmax probabilities sum to one, as does the one-hot target.

  3. Every component approaches zero; the example contributes almost no gradient.

  4. It likely computes exp(logits) or log(softmax(logits)) directly instead of using stable logsumexp or fused cross-entropy.

  5. Use the total number of non-ignored target tokens:

\[N_{\text{valid}} = \sum_{b,t}\mathbf 1[y_{b,t}\neq\text{ignore\_index}]\]
