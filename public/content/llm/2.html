🚀 Starting point

A loss is computed in four stages: (1) logits from the model, (2) a stable log-probability of each target, (3) masking of positions that shouldn’t count, (4) reduction to one number. Most bugs in this book are a mistake in stage 3 or 4.

✏️ Derivation: one example by hand

Logits \(z=(2,1,0)\), true class \(y=0\).

Step 1. Stabilize: subtract \(m=\max z=2\): \(z-m=(0,-1,-2)\). Softmax is unchanged, because the \(e^{-m}\) cancels in numerator and denominator.

Step 2. \(\LSE(z)=m+\log(e^0+e^{-1}+e^{-2})=2+\log(1.5032)=2.4076\).

Step 3. \(\ell=\LSE(z)-z_y=2.4076-2=\mathbf{0.4076}\).

Step 4. Probabilities \(p=e^{z-\LSE}=(0.665,0.245,0.090)\). Check: \(-\log0.665=0.408\).

Step 5. Gradient \(p-\onehot(0)=(-0.335,\,0.245,\,0.090)\). It sums to 0: pushing the true logit up and the others down by the same total.

Without step 1, logits like \((1000,999)\) give \(e^{1000}=\infty\) in float32 and the loss becomes NaN. Every library implementation subtracts the max.

✏️ Derivation: a batch with masking and reduction

Four token positions with per-token losses \(\ell=(0.41,1.20,0.05,2.30)\) and mask \(M=(1,1,1,0)\) (the last is padding).

Step 1. Masked sum \(S=\sum_tM_t\ell_t=0.41+1.20+0.05=1.66\).

Step 2. Valid count \(N=\sum_tM_t=3\).

Step 3. Mean loss \(L=S/N=0.553\). Dividing by 4 instead gives \(0.415\), silently underweighting this batch.

Step 4. Gradient of each valid token’s loss is scaled by \(1/N\), and padding gets exactly 0.

⚙️ Algorithm: computing a classification or language-model loss
Input: logits \(Z\in\mathbb R^{N\times C}\), targets \(y\in\{0..C-1\}^N\), mask \(M\in\{0,1\}^N\)
\(m_i\gets\max_cZ_{ic}\) ▷ stability shift, per row
\(\LSE_i\gets m_i+\log\sum_ce^{Z_{ic}-m_i}\)
\(\ell_i\gets\LSE_i-Z_{i,y_i}\) ▷ \(-\log p_{i,y_i}\)
\(L\gets\sum_iM_i\ell_i\,/\,\sum_iM_i\) ▷ masked mean
backward: \(\partial L/\partial Z_{i,:}=M_i\big(\operatorname{softmax}(Z_i)-\onehot(y_i)\big)/\sum_jM_j\)
⚙️ Algorithm: one training step (the loop every session refines)
Input: model \(f_\theta\), batch \((X,y,M)\), learning rate \(\eta\)
\(Z\gets f_\theta(X)\) ▷ forward
\(L\gets\) masked mean loss (algorithm above)
\(g\gets\nabla_\theta L\) ▷ backward: VJPs from the loss to every parameter
optionally clip \(g\) (Session 21)
\(\theta\gets\theta-\eta\,g\) ▷ or an optimizer: momentum, AdamW (Sessions 4, 15)
clear \(g\) before the next step ▷ PyTorch accumulates gradients
🔑 Key ideas

  • Loss \(=\) negative log-likelihood. Choose the distribution (Bernoulli, categorical, Gaussian), take \(-\log\), average.

  • Gradient at the logits is always “prediction minus target”: \(\sigma(z)-y\) or \(p-\onehot(y)\).

  • Compute in log space with a max shift, mask what shouldn’t count, and divide by the number of things that do.
