The statistics behind the scores

✏️ Derivation: pass@k, its unbiased estimator, and pass\(^k\)

Step 1. pass@k: the probability that at least one of \(k\) attempts succeeds. For a task with solve rate \(p\): \(1-(1-p)^k\).

Step 2. Draw \(n\ge k\) samples, \(c\) correct. The plug-in \(1-(1-c/n)^k\) is biased low (the function is concave in \(c/n\), so by Jensen its mean is below its value at the mean).

Step 3. Unbiased: pick \(k\) of the \(n\) samples at random; the chance that all are wrong is \(\binom{n-c}k/\binom nk\). So \(\widehat{\text{pass@}k}=1-\binom{n-c}k\big/\binom nk\).

Step 4. pass\(^k\) (from \(\tau\)-bench): the probability that all \(k\) attempts succeed, \(p^k\), estimated by \(\binom ck/\binom nk\). pass@k rises with \(k\); pass\(^k\) falls. A product needs pass\(^k\): users notice the one time in four it fails.

✏️ Derivation: The time-horizon metric

Step 1. Each task has a length \(t\): how long a skilled human takes. Model \(P(\text{success})=\sigma(\alpha-\beta\log_2t)\).

Step 2. Fit \(\alpha,\beta\) by logistic regression (Newton’s method, Part III). The 50% horizon is where \(\alpha-\beta\log_2t=0\): \(t_{50}=2^{\alpha/\beta}\).

Step 3. Bootstrap the tasks for an interval. METR reports that this horizon has been doubling roughly every seven months; it turns “score on benchmark X” into “length of task it can do”, which is comparable across benchmarks and years.

"""Agents ch. 'Benchmarks': pass@k and pass^k estimators, compounding errors, benchmark noise,
a METR-style time-horizon fit, and cost per solved task."""
import math

import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

# 1. pass@k from n samples per task with c correct. Unbiased: 1 - C(n-c, k)/C(n, k). Plug-in: 1 - (1-c/n)^k.
tasks, n, k = 2000, 20, 5
p = rng.beta(0.6, 0.9, tasks)                       # true per-task solve rates vary a lot
c = rng.binomial(n, p)
truth = np.mean(1 - (1 - p) ** k)
unbiased = np.mean([1 - math.comb(n - ci, k) / math.comb(n, k) for ci in c])
plug = np.mean(1 - (1 - c / n) ** k)
print(f"[pass@k] k={k}, n={n}: truth {truth:.4f}   unbiased estimator {unbiased:.4f}   plug-in {plug:.4f} (biased low)")

# 2. pass^k (tau-bench): all k independent tries succeed. Unbiased: C(c, k)/C(n, k).
print("[pass^k] k   pass@k   pass^k     (same agent: pass@1 = %.3f)" % p.mean())
for kk in (1, 2, 4, 8):
    pa = np.mean([1 - math.comb(n - ci, kk) / math.comb(n, kk) for ci in c])
    pw = np.mean([math.comb(ci, kk) / math.comb(n, kk) for ci in c])
    print(f"[pass^k] {kk}   {pa:.3f}    {pw:.3f}")

# 3. Compounding: a task of s steps, each right with prob 0.95. With a check that catches a wrong step
#    with prob d and up to 3 attempts, the effective per-step success rises.
q, d = 0.95, 0.9
# A step fails if a wrong attempt slips past the check, or all r attempts are wrong and caught.
miss = lambda r: sum(((1 - q) * d) ** i for i in range(r)) * (1 - q) * (1 - d) + ((1 - q) * d) ** r
print("[compound] steps   no checks   check+retry (3 tries, 90% detection)")
for s in (10, 30, 100):
    print(f"[compound] {s:4d}     {q ** s:.3f}       {(1 - miss(3)) ** s:.3f}")

# 4. Benchmark noise: SWE-bench Verified has 500 tasks.
for acc in (0.5, 0.7):
    print(f"[noise] {acc:.0%} on 500 tasks: 95% CI +- {1.96 * math.sqrt(acc * (1 - acc) / 500) * 100:.1f} points")

# 5. METR-style time horizon: P(success) is logistic in log2(human minutes). Fit and find the 50% point.
dur = 2 ** rng.uniform(0, math.log2(960), 400)                   # 1 minute to 16 hours
x = np.log2(dur)
y = (rng.random(400) < 1 / (1 + np.exp(0.8 * (x - math.log2(120))))).astype(float)


def fit(x, y):
    X = np.c_[np.ones_like(x), x]
    w = np.zeros(2)
    for _ in range(25):
        pr = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-X @ w))
        w -= np.linalg.solve(X.T @ (X * (pr * (1 - pr))[:, None]) + 1e-9 * np.eye(2), X.T @ (pr - y))
    return 2 ** (-w[0] / w[1])


h = fit(x, y)
boots = [fit(x[i], y[i]) for i in (rng.integers(0, 400, 400) for _ in range(300))]
lo, hi = np.percentile(boots, [5, 95])
print(f"[horizon] true 50% horizon 120 min; fitted {h:.0f} min (90% bootstrap CI {lo:.0f}-{hi:.0f})")

# 6. Cost per solved task: retry until the harness's verifier passes (up to 4 tries).
for name, cost, sr in (("large model", 1.20, 0.70), ("small model", 0.15, 0.35)):
    solved = 1 - (1 - sr) ** 4
    exp_cost = sum(cost * (1 - sr) ** i for i in range(4))
    print(f"[cost] {name}: ${cost:.2f}/attempt, {sr:.0%} per try -> {solved:.0%} solved within 4 tries, "
          f"${exp_cost / solved:.2f} per solved task")

Reading a leaderboard

  • Harness and budget: which scaffold, how many attempts, how many tokens or dollars per task, with or without a verifier picking the best attempt.

  • Noise: on 500 tasks the 95% interval is about \(\pm4\) points; gaps of 1–2 points are not differences. Compare paired, per task.

  • Contamination: public repositories and questions leak into pretraining data. Prefer post-cutoff or held-out sets.

  • Cost per solved task: a cheaper model with retries can beat a better one per dollar (the output: $0.43 vs $1.71).

  • Your own eval beats any public one: 50–200 real tasks from your work, scored automatically.
