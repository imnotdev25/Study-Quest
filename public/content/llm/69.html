🚀 Starting point

Advantage normalization rescales advantages to mean 0 and variance 1 so the policy gradient has a predictable size. It is an affine transform: it preserves the order of advantages but can flip the sign of some. The statistics must come from every valid token of the rollout, not from a minibatch or from padding.

Total: 20 minutes

Given masked rollout advantages:

\[A,M\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T}, \qquad M_{bt}\in\{0,1\}\]

compute statistics over all \(N\) valid rollout tokens:

\[N=\sum_{b,t}M_{bt}\]

\[\mu_A= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{b,t}M_{bt}A_{bt}\]

\[\sigma_A^2= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{b,t}M_{bt}(A_{bt}-\mu_A)^2\]

Then normalize:

\[\tilde A_{bt} = \frac{A_{bt}-\mu_A} {\sqrt{\sigma_A^2}+\epsilon}\]

This makes the valid advantages approximately zero-mean and unit-variance, stabilizing the actor-gradient scale.

Important distinctions:

  • Normalization can change individual advantage signs because it subtracts the rollout mean.

  • Compute statistics once over the rollout, then reuse them for every PPO minibatch and epoch.

  • Per-minibatch normalization makes the update depend on how samples were partitioned.

  • A one-element minibatch produces a centered advantage of zero—and therefore no policy gradient.

  • Do not identically normalize critic returns; the value model must predict the return’s actual scale.

Distributed workers need global count, sum, and sum of squares. For large or low-precision values, accumulate statistics in FP32/FP64 or use a stable variance algorithm.

✏️ Derivation: what normalization does, and the minibatch trap

Step 1. \(\tilde A=\frac{A-\mu}{\sigma+\epsilon}\) with masked \(\mu=\frac{\sum MA}{\sum M}\), \(\sigma^2=\frac{\sum M(A-\mu)^2}{\sum M}\). Then \(\sum M\tilde A\approx0\) and \(\frac{\sum M\tilde A^2}{\sum M}\approx1\).

Step 2. Policy gradient scales linearly with \(A\), so dividing by \(\sigma\) is like dividing the learning rate by \(\sigma\): a stable step size whatever the reward scale.

Step 3. Subtracting \(\mu\) is a constant baseline over the batch (Session 28), which doesn’t bias the gradient in expectation.

Step 4. Per-minibatch statistics use a different \(\mu\) in each minibatch, so the same raw advantage can be positive in one and negative in another.

Step 5. Extreme case: a one-element minibatch gives \(\tilde A=0\) always: no learning signal.

Step 6. Padding included: padded zeros drag \(\mu\) toward 0 and shrink \(\sigma\), distorting every real advantage.

⚙️ Algorithm: rollout-level advantage normalization
Input: advantages \(A\) and mask \(M\) for the entire rollout (all ranks)
\(N\gets\sum M\); \(\mu\gets\sum MA/N\); \(\sigma\gets\sqrt{\sum M(A-\mu)^2/N}\) ▷ all-reduce sums
\(\tilde A\gets M\odot(A-\mu)/(\sigma+10^{-8})\), detached
only then split into minibatches; reuse \(\tilde A\) for every epoch

Solution — open after attempting

def normalize_advantages(advantages, mask):
    valid = advantages[mask]

    mean = valid.mean()
    variance = valid.var(unbiased=False)
    std = variance.sqrt()

    normalized = torch.zeros_like(advantages)

    normalized[mask] = (
        valid - mean
    ) / (std + 1e-8)

    return normalized, mean, std

When [8,7,6] is normalized by itself, its mean is \(7\). The centered values are:

\[[1,0,-1]\]

so the raw positive advantage 6 becomes negative after local centering. It is below its minibatch’s average even though it remains above zero in absolute terms.

At ratio one:

\[L=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_t r_t\tilde A_t\]

and:

\[\frac{\partial r_t}{\partial\ell_{\text{new},t}} =r_t=1\]

Therefore:

\[\frac{\partial L} {\partial\ell_{\text{new},t}} = -\frac{\tilde A_t}{N}\]

Different locally normalized advantages directly produce different policy gradients. Global normalization avoids making the objective depend on arbitrary minibatch boundaries.

Check answers:

  1. Over every valid action token in the intended normalization population—normally the complete rollout batch, aggregated across workers.

  2. Zero: \[\frac{A-A}{\sqrt0+\epsilon}=0\] so that minibatch contributes no actor gradient.

  3. Centering measures advantage relative to the population mean. A positive value below that mean becomes negative after subtraction.

  4. Check whether each worker or minibatch is independently computing its own advantage mean and variance.

  5. Advantages are fixed rollout estimates. Allowing gradients through their value or reward computations introduces unintended optimization paths.
