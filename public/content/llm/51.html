🚀 Starting point

The question: with a fixed compute budget \(C\), how should it be split between model size \(N\) and training tokens \(D\)? Hoffmann et al. (2022) fit the loss as a sum of an irreducible term and two finite-size penalties: \(L(N,D)=E+\frac{A}{N^\alpha}+\frac{B}{D^\beta}\), with \(\alpha\approx0.34\), \(\beta\approx0.28\).

✏️ Derivation: the compute-optimal allocation

Step 1. Constraint: \(C=6ND\Rightarrow D=C/(6N)\). Substitute: \(L(N)=E+AN^{-\alpha}+B(6N/C)^{\beta}\).

Step 2. Minimize over \(N\): \(\frac{dL}{dN}=-\alpha AN^{-\alpha-1}+\beta B\,6^\beta C^{-\beta}N^{\beta-1}=0\).

Step 3. Rearrange: \(N^{\alpha+\beta}=\frac{\alpha A}{\beta B}\Big(\frac C6\Big)^{\beta}\), so \(\boxed{N^*\propto C^{\beta/(\alpha+\beta)}}\) and \(D^*=C/(6N^*)\propto C^{\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)}\).

Step 4. With the fitted values both exponents are close to \(0.5\): scale parameters and tokens equally. The empirical rule of thumb is \(D^*\approx20N^*\).

Step 5. Example: \(C=10^{24}\) FLOPs. \(C=6N(20N)=120N^2\Rightarrow N=\sqrt{10^{24}/120}=91\)B parameters, \(D=1.8\)T tokens. GPT-3 (175B on 300B tokens) was heavily under-trained by this measure.

Step 6. After Chinchilla: inference cost depends on \(N\) only, so models served to many users are now trained far past \(20N\) (Llama 3 8B saw about 15T tokens, roughly 1,900 per parameter). Compute-optimal for training isn’t optimal for deployment.

Chinchilla’s fitted \(L(N,D)\) (\(E=1.69\), \(A=406.4\), \(B=410.7\)) along \(D=C/6N\). Each budget has a best size (dots), and the best size grows with \(C\). This particular fit puts the optimum at 60–100 tokens per parameter. The “\(\approx20\)” rule comes from the paper’s two other estimation methods; the fits disagree, which is why you’ll see both numbers quoted.
