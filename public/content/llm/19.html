🚀 Starting point

Real-life version. A dot product is a “how much do we agree” score: two people’s ratings on the same movies, multiplied and summed. Softmax turns those scores into a vote share. Temperature is how decisive the room is: low temperature, the loudest opinion wins; high temperature, everyone gets a say.

Dot product and cosine

\(q\cdot k=\lVert q\rVert\lVert k\rVert\cos\theta\). Cosine measures direction only. The raw dot product also rewards length, which is why in the output below a key three times longer gets three times the score even though its direction is identical. Attention uses raw dot products, so the norms of queries and keys matter. That is the problem QK-norm (Toolkit II) solves.

Softmax and temperature

\(\operatorname{softmax}(z/T)_i=e^{z_i/T}/\sum_je^{z_j/T}\). Adding a constant to all logits cancels between numerator and denominator, so we subtract \(\max z\) before exponentiating to avoid overflow. As \(T\to0\) softmax becomes argmax; as \(T\to\infty\) it becomes uniform.

✏️ Derivation: Why attention divides by \(\sqrt d\)

Step 1. Let \(q,k\in\mathbb R^d\) have independent entries with mean 0 and variance 1.

Step 2. \(\operatorname{Var}(q_ik_i)=\mathbb E[q_i^2]\mathbb E[k_i^2]=1\), and the \(d\) terms are independent, so \(\operatorname{Var}(q\cdot k)=d\).

Step 3. Standard deviation \(\sqrt d\): at \(d=256\) scores spread over \(\pm16\), softmax saturates, and its gradients vanish. Dividing by \(\sqrt d\) brings the spread back to 1.

Broadcasting

Align shapes from the right; each dimension must be equal or 1, and size-1 dimensions are stretched without copying. \((5,1,3)-(1,4,3)\to(5,4,3)\) computes every pairwise difference. The second method in the code uses \(\lVert a-b\rVert^2=\lVert a\rVert^2+\lVert b\rVert^2-2a\cdot b\), which turns the work into one matrix multiply, the form GPUs like.

L1 vs L2 norms

\(\lVert x\rVert_1=\sum|x_i|\) and \(\lVert x\rVert_2=\sqrt{\sum x_i^2}\). The unit L1 ball is a diamond with corners on the axes, so an L1-constrained optimum tends to land on a corner, where some coordinates are zero. The L2 ball is round, so it has no preferred corner. That picture is the geometric version of the soft-threshold derivation in the previous chapter. In LLMs, L2 shows up as weight decay and gradient clipping by norm; RMSNorm divides by \(\lVert x\rVert_2/\sqrt d\).

"""Toolkit ch. 2: dot products, softmax temperature, broadcasting."""
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(1)

# Dot product vs cosine: the dot product also rewards length.
q = np.array([1.0, 2.0, 0.0])
K = np.array([[1.0, 2.0, 0.1],    # same direction
              [3.0, 6.0, 0.0],    # same direction, 3x longer
              [-2.0, 1.0, 0.0]])  # orthogonal
dots = K @ q
cos = dots / (np.linalg.norm(K, axis=1) * np.linalg.norm(q))
print(f"[dot] q.k    = {np.round(dots, 2)}")
print(f"[dot] cosine = {np.round(cos, 3)}")


def softmax(z, T=1.0):
    z = np.asarray(z) / T
    e = np.exp(z - z.max())          # subtract max: same result, no overflow
    return e / e.sum()


z = np.array([2.0, 1.0, 0.1])
for T in (0.25, 1.0, 4.0):
    print(f"[softmax] T={T:<4}  {np.round(softmax(z, T), 3)}")
print(f"[softmax] shift-invariant: {np.allclose(softmax(z), softmax(z + 1000))}")

# Why attention divides by sqrt(d): q.k for random unit-variance vectors has variance d.
for d in (16, 256):
    s = (rng.normal(size=(10_000, d)) * rng.normal(size=(10_000, d))).sum(1)
    print(f"[scale] d={d:3d}  std(q.k)={s.std():6.2f}  std(q.k/sqrt d)={(s / np.sqrt(d)).std():.2f}")

# Broadcasting: all pairwise squared distances, no loops.
A, B = rng.normal(size=(5, 3)), rng.normal(size=(4, 3))
D1 = ((A[:, None, :] - B[None, :, :]) ** 2).sum(-1)              # (5,1,3)-(1,4,3) -> (5,4,3)
D2 = (A**2).sum(1)[:, None] + (B**2).sum(1)[None, :] - 2 * A @ B.T  # (5,1)+(1,4)-(5,4)
print(f"[broadcast] D shape {D1.shape}, two methods agree: {np.allclose(D1, D2)}")
