🚀 Starting point

Three ways to use an LLM to make pre-training data: generate textbooks from topic seeds, rephrase real web pages into cleaner or more varied forms, or extract question–answer pairs from them. Rephrasing is the most popular now because the facts come from real documents while the style and density come from the model.

  • Phi-1 / “Textbooks Are All You Need” (Microsoft, 2023): about 1B tokens of GPT-3.5 textbooks and exercises, plus filtered code, let a 1.3B model beat much larger ones on HumanEval.

  • Cosmopedia (HuggingFace, 2024): about 25B tokens (30M files) of synthetic textbooks, blog posts and stories from Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, seeded by web pages and curated topic lists, for diversity. Cosmopedia v2 is part of smollm-corpus.

  • WRAP (Apple/CMU, 2024): rephrase web documents “like Wikipedia” or “as question–answer”; mixing rephrased with real data trains about 3\(\times\) faster to the same perplexity.

  • Nemotron-CC-v2 (NVIDIA, 2025; dataset card read this week): 3.36T tokens of real English crawl, plus 1.26T tokens of synthetic rephrasings and 0.69T of “Diverse QA” (also translated into 15 languages, 0.56T), generated with Mistral-Nemo-12B and Qwen3-30B-A3B using five prompt styles.

The rephrase function in synth.py (listing in the next chapter) does this with three prompts: wiki, qa and textbook. Mix its output with the original documents rather than replacing them.
