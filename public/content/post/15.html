🚀 Starting point

Synthetic data is training data written by an LLM. It has gone from a trick to the default: SmolTalk and Tulu 3’s SFT mixes are mostly synthetic, Nemotron-CC-v2 includes about 2.5T tokens of LLM-rephrased web text, and almost every open reasoning model is trained on traces sampled from a stronger one. It’s cheap, controllable and scalable. Its risks are low diversity, inherited errors, and model collapse when models train on their own outputs.

Every synthetic-data recipe in this part has these four stages. The filter is where quality comes from.

✏️ Derivation: model collapse in one line of math

Step 1. Fit a Gaussian to \(n\) samples, sample \(n\) new points from the fit, refit, repeat. Each refit’s variance estimate \(\hat\sigma^2_{t+1}\) is \(\hat\sigma^2_t\cdot\chi^2_{n-1}/(n-1)\), a random factor with mean slightly below 1 for the usual MLE.

Step 2. Products of such factors drift toward 0: tails disappear first, then variance collapses (Shumailov et al., 2024). The LLM version: rare facts and styles vanish after a few generations of training on outputs.

Step 3. Mitigation: accumulate rather than replace. Keep the real data and add synthetic data each round; under this regime the error stays bounded (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024). In practice: mix synthetic with real data, filter hard, and generate from diverse seeds.
