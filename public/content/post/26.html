From an arXiv search for safety alignment and post-training, newest first (titles read October 2026; I read abstracts only):

  • Safety earlier in training: “Safety Mid-Training” (2610.26809), “Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection” (2606.19168), and “Can Native Safety Travel? Reusing Safety Pretraining Across Models via On-Policy Distillation” (2610.27555).

  • Distillation for safety: “Constitutional On-Policy Safe Distillation” (2606.03089) and “DUET: Dual-Teacher On-Policy Distillation for Prohibition Compliance” (2608.14644).

  • Robustness to fine-tuning: SLDR (2610.10345), “CSULoRA: Closest Safe Update Low-Rank Adaptation” (2605.30640), and “Patcher: Post-Hoc Patching of Backdoored LLMs” (2606.02995).

  • Mechanisms of refusal: “Attention Sees, MLP Refuses” (2610.13078) and the refusal-geometry papers above.

  • Agent safety: “AgentBoundary: Counterfactual Evaluation of Safety in Tool-Using LLM Agents” (2609.33658) and “Why Do AI Agents Break Rules?” (2608.12323).

  • Balancing objectives: “Don’t Mix Rewards, Mix Policies” (2607.29246) on helpfulness–safety RL, and “Does Reasoning Preserve Alignment?” (2606.11046) on large reasoning models.

🔑 Key ideas

  • Alignment is a loop: spec, data, training, evaluation, repeat.

  • Report attack success with over-refusal, with intervals, on matched prompts.

  • Safety from post-training alone is shallow; layer it with guards and push it earlier into training.
